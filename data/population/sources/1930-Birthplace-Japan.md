# 出生地 — where the people counted in Japan proper were born, 1930

From 内閣統計局『昭和五年国勢調査最終報告書』pp. 68–69 —
<https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1281995/1/30>.

Two tables about the same question. The census asked everybody enumerated in
内地 where they were born; these are the ones whose answer was not 内地.

## Born in the 外地, p. 68

| 出生地 | | 總數 |
|---|---|---:|
| 朝鮮 | Chōsen (Korea) | 434,934 |
| 臺灣 | Taiwan (Formosa) | 30,264 |
| 樺太 | Karafuto | 7,419 |
| 關東州(州内) | Kwantung Leased Territory | 12,445 |
| 南洋群島 | South Seas Mandate | 735 |
| **總數** | **All of the 外地** | **485,797** |

The five sum to the printed 總數 exactly. Four hundred and thirty-five thousand
of the 485,797 were born in Korea, which is nine in ten — the Korean migration
into Japan proper is most of what this table is.

## Born in a foreign country, p. 69

The same page gives 114,862 born outside the empire altogether, broken down as
below. The parts sum to that total exactly, and each continent's parts sum to
its own printed subtotal.

**These are birthplaces, not nationalities.** The two largest entries are the
United States at 27,288 and Hawaii at 13,504, and those are overwhelmingly the
children of Japanese emigrants coming back — *kibei* — rather than Americans
living in Japan. A reader who takes this table for a count of foreigners will
read the Pacific exactly backwards.
