# 民族省別人口 — Manchukuo by nationality, May 1943

**第二表 年齡民族省別人口**, from 滿洲國『臨時國勢調査報告』pp. 6–7 —
<https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/3459064/1/15>. The image is at
`images/1943-Manchukuo-table2-nationality.png`.

## What is here, and what is not

The crop covers the head of the table and its **總數** block — the whole of
Manchukuo, and its men and women — with every column the table carries. **The
province rows below it are outside the crop and are not transcribed.** Adding
them needs the rest of pp. 6–7.

## The categories, as the source has them

The report divides everybody in Manchukuo four ways at the top level, and the
first two again beneath that. The map's tables give them in English with the
source's own words beside them.

| the source | here |
|---|---|
| 滿洲人 | Manchurian nationals — the state's own subjects, and its own term |
| 　滿洲族人 | Manchu |
| 　漢人 | Han Chinese |
| 　蒙古人 | Mongolian |
| 　回教人 | Hui Muslim |
| 　其他 | Other |
| 日本人 | Japanese nationals |
| 　內地人 | Japanese (naichijin) |
| 　朝鮮人 | Koreans |
| 　其他 | Other |
| 第三國人 | Third-country nationals |
| 無國籍人 | Stateless |

The last is worth a sentence. 69,180 people in Manchukuo held no nationality at
all in May 1943, and it is the one category with **more women than men** —
35,125 to 34,055, in a country running 123.9 men to a hundred women overall.
Most of them were White Russians, stateless since 1921 and concentrated in
Harbin and along the eastern line.

## Checked against the source's own arithmetic

Every identity this block can be asked for holds, in all three rows:

| check | 總數 | 男 | 女 |
|---|---|---|---|
| the five 滿洲人 parts sum to the 滿洲人 total | 40,858,473 ✓ | 22,600,322 ✓ | 18,258,151 ✓ |
| the three 日本人 parts sum to the 日本人 total | 2,271,495 ✓ | 1,271,517 ✓ | 999,978 ✓ |
| 滿洲人 + 日本人 + 第三國人 + 無國籍人 | 43,202,880 ✓ | 23,908,082 ✓ | 19,294,798 ✓ |
| 男 + 女 = 總數 | in every one of the thirteen columns ✓ |

And the grand total agrees with 第一表: 43,202,880.

## Reading notes

- The Han were 85.3 per cent of Manchukuo's people and the Manchu 6.2, in the
  state that took its name from them.
- 朝鮮人 outnumber 內地人 by nearly two to one: 1,450,384 Koreans against
  819,614 Japanese from the home islands, both counted here as 日本人 because
  Korea was under Japanese rule and its people were Japanese nationals.
