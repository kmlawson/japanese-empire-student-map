# 市ノ人口 — the cities of Japan proper, 1930

From 内閣統計局『昭和五年国勢調査最終報告書』p. 16 —
<https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1281995/1/30>. The 市 of 内地 at the census of
1 October 1930, largest first. The page lists them down to 川崎 at 104,351;
everything below that is not transcribed here, and the map's smaller Japanese
cities keep the rounded figures they already carried.

**東京市 is not 東京府.** The city is 2,070,913 and the prefecture 5,408,678:
the fifteen wards were still the whole of the city in 1930, and the
amalgamation that took in the surrounding twenty wards came in 1932. That is
why **大阪市 is the larger of the two here**, at 2,453,573 — a fact worth
having on a map of 1930, and one that the 1940 figures reverse.

## The four sizes

The gazetteer has drawn dots at four weights since it was built — 2.5, 3.4, 4.4
and 5.8 screen pixels — and this table is what decides which weight a Japanese
city gets on the 1930 map:

| icon | cities |
|---|---|
| largest | Ōsaka, Tōkyō |
| large | Nagoya, Kobe, Kyōto, Yokohama |
| medium | the remaining twenty-two on this page, Hiroshima 270,417 down to Kawasaki 104,351 |
| small | every other Japanese city the map carries |

Hiroshima was `large` before and is `medium` now, which is the only demotion:
at 270,417 it is a third of Yokohama and would have been drawn the same size.
The cities that were `medium` on figures rounded to the thousand — Gifu,
Hamamatsu, Toyohashi, Ōmuta, Toyama, Tokushima, Matsuyama, Kōchi, Kōfu,
Maizuru, Aomori, Shimonoseki, Fukui, Chiba, Kokura, Utsunomiya, Maebashi,
Morioka, Ōita, Nagano and the rest — are `small`, because none of them is on
this page.
