# 年齢別人口 — the three age groups, Japan 1930

From 内閣統計局『昭和五年国勢調査最終報告書』pp. 40–41 —
<https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1281995/1/30>. The source gives **人口千中**, each
group per thousand of the prefecture's own population. That is what is
transcribed below, in the left three columns, because it is what the page says.

**The card and the table show it as a percentage** — 366 read as 36.6% — which
is the same figure said in the unit a reader already has. It is a move of the
decimal point and nothing else: no precision is gained and none is lost, and
the derived column is here beside the printed one so the two can be checked
against each other.

What the map does *not* do is turn a share into a count. Multiplying a rounded
per-thousand by a population would invent four figures of precision the census
did not print.

**Checked by their own arithmetic.** All forty-eight rows sum to 1,000 within
the rounding — the largest departure is one part in a thousand, which is what
three rounded shares of a whole can do. Forty-eight rows landing there by
accident is not possible, so the reading is sound.

Japan proper is 366 / 560 / 74. The cities are young and working — 東京 318 /
639 / 43 and 大阪 307 / 643 / 50, a fifth more of the country's working ages
than the average and barely half the old people — and the far west and the
islands are the other way: 島根 has 111 in a thousand over sixty against
東京's 43.
