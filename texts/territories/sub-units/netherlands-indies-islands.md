> islands and states, matched by the ring the pointer is over

## Sumatra

Rubber, tobacco and above all the oil of Palembang, which was taken by parachute assault on 14 February 1942 before the refineries could be destroyed.

# Island by island

## Java

The centre of the colony and of its population: two thirds of the Indies lived here. The Dutch surrendered on 8 March 1942, and the Japanese levied the <em>rōmusha</em> labour drafts from it.

## Madura

Salt, and cattle, and the island's own princely houses.

## Borneo

Oil at Balikpapan and Tarakan, which was the reason the southern advance came this way at all. Taken in January and February 1942.

## Sulawesi

Celebes, taken between January and February 1942, and put under naval rather than army administration for the rest of the war.

## Bali

Taken on 19 February 1942. The action in Badung Strait the following night went the other way: four Japanese destroyers beat off a much larger Allied force and sank a Dutch destroyer.

## Lombok

Between Bali and Sumbawa, and the strait through which the Allied ships that got away from Java escaped south.

## Sumbawa

A dry island of horses and sandalwood, and of Tambora, whose eruption in 1815 cooled the whole world.

## Flores

Taken in 1942 and used for an airstrip; otherwise left to itself.

## Sumba

Horses and ikat weaving, and little else the occupation wanted.

## WestTimor

The Dutch half of Timor, taken on 20 February 1942 with a parachute landing behind the Australian force at Koepang.

## Halmahera

Bypassed in 1944 when the Americans took Morotai beside it instead, and its garrison was left to starve.

## Seram

Ceram, and the oil at Bula on its eastern end.

## Buru

A quiet island off Ceram, later notorious as an Indonesian prison colony.

## Bangka

Tin, worked by Chinese labour since the eighteenth century. Off its coast in February 1942 Japanese troops machine-gunned twenty-two Australian nurses who had survived a sinking; one lived.

## Belitung

Tin, like Banka beside it, and worked the same way.

## Nias

Off the west coast of Sumatra, and famous for its megaliths and stone-jumping.

## WestNewGuinea

The Dutch half of New Guinea, hardly administered before the war and the ground the Americans came back through in 1944 — Hollandia, Biak, Sansapor.
