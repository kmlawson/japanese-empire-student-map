> the Kuriles, north-east to south-west. Japanese throughout both dates: the
> northern islands are where the Aleutian operation was mounted from, and the
> four southernmost are the ones still disputed with Russia

## Shumshu (Shimushu)

The northernmost island, heavily garrisoned and facing Kamchatka. Soviet troops landed here on 18 August 1945, three days after the surrender, in the last battle of the war.

# Island by island

## Paramushir (Paramushiro)

The naval and air base from which the Aleutian operation was mounted, and the target of American bombers flying from Attu after 1943.

## Alaid (Araito)

A volcanic cone off Shumshu, uninhabited but for a fishery station.

## Makanrushi (Makanru)

Uninhabited, and used only by fishing crews in season.

## Onekotan

Uninhabited but for its wartime garrison, and two great calderas, one holding a lake with an island in it.

## Kharimkotan (Harimukotan)

Uninhabited; its 1933 eruption swept the shore.

## Ekarma

Uninhabited.

## Shiashkotan (Shasukotan)

A small settlement and a fox farm, abandoned when the islands changed hands.

## Matua (Matsuwa)

An airfield in the middle of the chain, bombed from the Aleutians and bypassed.

## Rasshua (Rasuwa)

Uninhabited.

## Ketoy (Ketoi)

Uninhabited.

## Simushir (Shimushiru)

A garrison, and a flooded crater at Broughton Bay that makes one of the few
sheltered anchorages in the chain.

## Chirpoy (Chirihoi)

Uninhabited.

## Urup (Uruppu)

Sea otter hunting ground, and Russian until the exchange of 1875.

## Kunashiri (Kunashir)

The southernmost large island, Japanese-settled and taken by Soviet troops on 1 September 1945. Japan claims it still.

## Shikotan

Taken by Soviet troops on 1 September 1945; its Japanese inhabitants were deported in 1947 and Japan claims it still.

## the Habomai Islands

A scatter of islets in sight of Hokkaidō, taken between 1 and 5 September 1945 — after the surrender — and claimed by Japan ever since.
