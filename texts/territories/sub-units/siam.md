> Siam's changwat, as they stood from 1933 to 1947 — seventy of them, and the
> map draws sixty-nine plus the two halves of Bangkok. Old romanisations, which
> is how period maps spell them, come first.
>
> The 1930 sheet is the weaker of the two and every entry says so. The changwat
> then sat inside the monthon, the fourteen circles of the Chakri reforms, which
> were abolished in 1933 and are not drawn; and nine provinces that existed in
> 1930 were abolished on 1 April 1932 into their neighbours, so their ground is
> inside the wrong outline here and cannot be got back out of a modern source.
> December 1942 is sound: seventy changwat on pre-war soil, plus the four the
> ceded territory was governed as, which this map draws as one atom.

## Thonburi

The right bank of the Chao Phraya, and a province of its own until 1971: the
capital King Taksin built after the fall of Ayutthaya in 1767, before Rama I
crossed the river and began Bangkok on the other side. Through both of this
map's dates it was the working half of the city — rice mills, sawmills, the
docks and the klong network — against Phra Nakhon's palaces and ministries
opposite. It is drawn here from the fifteen modern khet that descend from its
districts.
