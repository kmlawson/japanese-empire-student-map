> Manchukuo's own fourteen provinces, from 滿洲國地圖 1935. The key is the sheet's
> own romanisation, which is how a reader of 1935 met the name. Every one
> reads the same way: pinyin with its tones first, the sheet's romanisation
> after it in brackets, and the characters with their Japanese reading on the
> line below. No city and no gloss — Harbin and Shenyang are on the Cities
> layer, where a reader who wants them will look, and a province tooltip that
> names a city as well reads as though the province were the city's suburb.
> The four Hsing An provinces are the eastern Mongol leagues, which had been
> part of the Three Eastern Provinces before 1932 and were reorganised as
> Hinggan north, south, east and west. Je Hol is Rehe, taken from the
> Republic in 1933 — which is why this set belongs to the 1942 map alone: on
> the 1930 map Rehe is a Chinese province standing outside Manchuria, and
> these provincial boundaries did not exist. Manchukuo went on subdividing
> after this sheet — sixteen provinces by 1937 and nineteen by 1941 — so what
> is drawn is the scheme of 1935, not of the map's own date.
