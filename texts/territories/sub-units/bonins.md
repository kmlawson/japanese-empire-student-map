> Two groups under one administration. The Bonins proper — Chichijima,
> Hahajima, Mukojima — were claimed in 1876; the Volcano Islands 250 km further
> south, Iwo Jima among them, were annexed in 1891. Both were governed from
> Tokyo, and the card for the group says only the earlier date, which is why
> each island here says which of the two it belongs to.

## Chichijima

The administrative centre of the group and its only good harbour, Futami. The
first settlement was made in 1830 by a party of Americans, Europeans and
Hawaiians; Perry called in 1853 and wanted a coaling station on it. Japan
annexed the islands in 1876 and naturalised the settlers, whose descendants
live on the island still.

## Hahajima

The second settlement of the Bonins, forty kilometres south of Chichijima,
growing sugar cane and vegetables for the Tokyo market.

## Mukojima

The northernmost cluster of the Bonins, uninhabited, and grazed bare by the
goats landed on it in the nineteenth century.

## Iwo Jima (Iō-tō)

The name means sulphur island and the sulphur was worked from the 1890s,
alongside sugar and coca, by about a thousand settlers in two villages. It is
the only one of the Volcano Islands flat enough to build an airfield on, which
is what decided its history.

## Kita-Iō-tō

A cone with no harbour and no flat ground, farmed in terraces by a few hundred
people until they were taken off in 1944. Uninhabited since.

## Minami-Iō-tō

Cliffs on every side, never settled and never garrisoned. It is a nature
reserve now and almost nobody is allowed to land.
