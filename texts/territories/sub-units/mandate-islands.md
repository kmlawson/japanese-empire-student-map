> individual islands of the mandate

## Saipan

Sugar, and the largest Japanese civilian population in the mandate. The Americans landed on 15 June 1944 and took it by 9 July; hundreds of civilians killed themselves at Marpi Point rather than surrender.

# The islands, one by one

## Tinian

Taken between 24 July and 1 August 1944. Its airfields became the busiest in the world, and the atomic missions to Hiroshima and Nagasaki flew from them.

## Rota

Between Saipan and Guam, and never assaulted: its garrison was bypassed and held out until the surrender in 1945.

## Pagan

An airstrip in the northern Marianas, bombed and then left behind; the garrison was still there in 1945.

## Agrihan

A volcano in the northern Marianas with a few dozen people on it, bypassed entirely.

## Anatahan

Bypassed and forgotten. Some of its garrison refused to believe the war was over and did not give themselves up until June 1951.

## Babeldaob

The main island of Palau. After Peleliu the Americans left it alone, and its garrison of some twenty-five thousand was starving by 1945.

## Peleliu

Landed on 15 September 1944 in an operation expected to take four days; it took over two months and introduced the deep cave defence used again on Iwo Jima and Okinawa.

## Angaur

Phosphate, worked since the German period. Taken between 17 September and 22 October 1944, alongside Peleliu.

## Weno

Moen, the main island of Truk and the seat of the anchorage. Operation Hailstone wrecked the fleet base here on 17–18 February 1944, after which Truk was bypassed and left to rot.

## Kwajalein

Taken between 31 January and 3 February 1944, with Majuro — the first assault on territory Japan had held since before the war, and the breach of the outer perimeter.

## Majuro

Taken without a fight on 31 January 1944 and turned into the fleet anchorage from which the drive across the central Pacific was mounted.

## Jaluit

The administrative centre of the Marshalls under both the German and the Japanese administrations. Bypassed in 1944, bombed, and starved to the surrender.

## Wotje

An air base bypassed in 1944 and bombed for the rest of the war; its garrison was never assaulted.

## Enewetak

Taken between 17 and 23 February 1944, which carried the Americans to the western edge of the Marshalls.

## Bikini

Never fought over. Its people were moved off in 1946 for the American nuclear tests and have never been able to return.

## Ebon

The southernmost of the Marshalls, and the first of them to be reached by missionaries. Bypassed in the war.
