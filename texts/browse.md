# China, Manchuria and the west

> Occupation and liberation dates where a city changed hands, and what it was
> there for where it did not. One or two sentences: these are context, not
> quiz places.

## paoting

On the Beijing–Hankou railway, and the seat of the military academy that trained a generation of Republican officers. Fell on 24 September 1937; Shijiazhuang, the junction south of it, went in October.

## tangshan

The Kailuan mines, the largest coal workings in north China and British-managed until Japan took them over in 1941.

## shanhaiguan

Where the Great Wall meets the sea and the Manchurian railway crosses into China proper. Taken on 1 January 1933, opening the Rehe campaign.

## datong

Coal, and the junction of the two railways the occupation ran on. Taken on 13 September 1937 and attached to the Japanese-sponsored government of northern Shanxi.

## taiyuan

Yan Xishan's capital for thirty years, with the arsenal and steelworks he built to keep Shanxi independent of everyone. Lin Biao's ambush at Pingxingguan in September 1937 and the battle of Xinkou in October delayed its fall until 9 November.

## kaifeng

The Northern Song capital, and a station on the Lunghai railway. Fell on 6 June 1938; the dikes at Huayuankou were cut three days later to stop the advance beyond it.

## luoyang

The Longhai railway city and one of the old capitals of China. Held out until 25 May 1944, and the December 1942 line of control leaves it outside the occupation. The Henan famine of 1942–43 killed some two million people in the country round it.

## zhengzhou

The junction of the north–south and east–west trunk railways, and the reason the Huayuankou dikes were cut. Taken briefly in October 1941, given up again, and held from April 1944.

## yantai

The treaty port of Yantai, known for lace, silk and the missionary school. Occupied in February 1938.

## zhenjiang

Where the Grand Canal meets the Yangtze, and the provincial capital of Jiangsu until 1937. Fell in December 1937.

## suzhou

Silk, gardens, and the Shanghai–Nanjing railway. Fell on 19 November 1937 in the pursuit from Shanghai.

## wuxi

The cotton and silk mill town of the lower Yangtze, second only to Shanghai in the region's industry. Fell on 25 November 1937.

## wuhu

The great rice market of the lower Yangtze. Fell on 10 December 1937, three days before Nanjing, cutting the river escape route from the capital.

## xuzhou

The crossing of the north–south and east–west trunk railways, and for that reason the object of the largest campaign of 1938. The Chinese victory at Taierzhuang in April held it until 19 May, and the army got away rather than be encircled.

## bengbu

On the Tianjin–Pukow railway where it crosses the Huai. Taken in February 1938 in the drive north towards Xuzhou.

## hefei

The seat of Anhui, on the road between the Yangtze and the Huai. Occupied in 1938, though the front bent round the Chinese pocket in the north-west of the province beyond it.

## anqing

The old provincial capital of Anhui, and a river port on the Yangtze. Fell on 12 June 1938, opening the campaign up the river to Hankou. The New Fourth Army Incident of January 1941 was fought in the mountains south of it.

## jiujiang

The tea port of Jiangxi and the outlet of the Poyang lake. Fell on 26 July 1938, halfway up the river to Hankou.

## yichang

Where cargo transferred from steamer to junk for the passage of the gorges. Taken on 12 June 1940 and held as the furthest Japanese post up the Yangtze, below the water that shielded Chongqing.

## hangzhou

Silk, and the southern end of the Grand Canal. Fell on 24 December 1937.

## ningbo

A treaty port from 1842 and a merchant city whose bankers ran much of Shanghai. Occupied in April 1941; Unit 731 dropped plague-infected fleas on it in 1940.

## wenzhou

A minor treaty port behind a mountain wall, taken and given up three times between 1941 and 1945. It lies outside the line of control drawn here for December 1942.

## nanchang

Where the Communist rising of 1 August 1927 gave the Red Army its founding date, and where Chiang launched the New Life Movement seven years later. Fell on 27 March 1939.

## changsha

The rice bowl of Hunan and a junction on the Guangzhou–Hankou line. Its own garrison burned it down by mistake in November 1938; it then beat off three Japanese offensives and fell at last on 18 June 1944.

## hengyang

The junction of the Guangzhou–Hankou and Hunan–Guangxi railways, and the airfield the Ichigo offensive was launched to take. Its garrison held for forty-seven days and surrendered on 8 August 1944.

## yueyang

The Tungting lake port on the Guangzhou–Hankou line, taken in November 1938 and the base from which every attack on Changsha was mounted.

## shaoguan

The wartime capital of Guangdong after Guangzhou fell, on the railway north to Hunan, and not taken until January 1945.

## guilin

A Nationalist air base among the limestone hills, and the refuge of the universities of the south. Destroyed by its own garrison and abandoned on 10 November 1944.

## wuzhou

Where the West River leaves Guangxi for Guangzhou, and the trade route between them. Taken in the 1944 offensive.

## nanning

The road and rail gate to Indochina, which is why it was taken in November 1939, given up in 1940, and taken again in 1944.

## shantou

A treaty port from 1860, the departure point for much of the Chinese emigration to Southeast Asia, and the outlet for Guangdong's sugar. Occupied on 21 June 1939.

## fuzhou

A treaty port from 1842 and the great tea shipping port of the nineteenth century. Taken in April 1941, retaken by Chinese forces that September, and taken again in October 1944.

## guiyang

The hinge of the road system of the south-west, where the routes from Chongqing, Kunming and Guangxi met. Never occupied: the Japanese advance of December 1944 reached Dushan, a hundred miles short of it, and turned back — the furthest inland the war ever came.

## kunming

The terminus of the Burma Road, of the railway from Haiphong, and after both were cut of the air route over the Hump. Peking University, Tsinghua and Nankai spent the war here as Lianda, the National Southwest Associated University. Never occupied.

## chengdu

The rice and silk capital of the Sichuan basin. Never occupied — and the four airfields built round it by hand in 1944 were the first from which B-29s bombed Japan.

## zunyi

Where the Communist leadership met in January 1935, in the middle of the Long March, and Mao emerged in charge of the party's military line.

## wanxian

The upper Yangtze port above the gorges, and the scene of a British naval bombardment in 1926 that did much to turn Chinese opinion against the treaty powers.

## dali

The old Nanzhao capital, on the Burma Road through western Yunnan along which the fighting came in 1944.

## baoji

The railhead of the line west from Sian, and the road to Gansu and the Soviet supply route.

## tianshui

On the Gansu corridor where the Lunghai railway ran out, a stage on the overland road that carried Soviet aid until 1941.

## lanzhou

Where the road to Xinjiang crosses the Yellow River, and the point through which Soviet aid and Soviet aircraft entered China. Bombed for it from 1937; never occupied.

## xining

The seat of the Ma family who governed Qinghai for the Republic, and the wool and hide market of the Tibetan borderland. Never occupied.

## yinchuan

The oasis capital of Ningxia on the upper Yellow River, held for the Republic by Ma Hongkui throughout. Never occupied.

## hohhot

The Inner Mongolian trade and lamasery city on the Beijing–Suiyuan railway. Taken on 14 October 1937 and renamed Kōwa; Demchugdongrub's Mongol government sat here before the seat moved to Zhangjiakou.

## baotou

The railhead of the Beijing–Suiyuan line and the wool market of the steppe. Taken in October 1937, and the western limit of Japanese control; the country beyond stayed with Fu Zuoyi.

## hohhot2

The oasis where the Edsin Gol dies in the Gobi, and the seat of the Ejine Torghut banner, administered from Ningxia. Khara-Khoto, the Tangut city Kozlov dug out of the sand in 1908, lies a short way downstream. Nothing to do with Mengchiang: the nearest ground that state put on its own maps is 240 km to the east, and this was never within its reach or Japan's.

## qiqihar

The seat of Heilongjiang and the junction of the Chinese Eastern Railway with the line north. Taken on 19 November 1931 after the fighting at the Nen bridges.

## jilincity

A timber and river city on the Songhua, taken in September 1931 when its provincial governor went over to the Japanese.

## mudanjiang

A garrison town and railway junction on the eastern line to Vladivostok, and the first objective of the Soviet armies in August 1945.

## jiamusi

The seat of Sankiang on the Songhua, and the country the Manchukuo settler schemes were pushed into hardest.

## fushun

The open-cast coal mine that fuelled the South Manchuria Railway and much of Japanese industry. Chinese labourers died here in tens of thousands.

## anshan

The Shōwa Steel Works, the largest ironworks in the empire outside Japan, and the target of the first B-29 raid on Manchuria in July 1944.

## jinzhou

The gate between Manchuria and the Wall, on the railway from Shenyang to Beijing. Taken on 3 January 1932, which put Japanese troops on the Wall; Harbin held out until February.

## yingkou

The old treaty port of Yingkou at the mouth of the Liao, once the outlet for Manchurian soya beans and long overtaken by Dalian.

## dandong

Where the railway crosses the Yalu into Korea — the bridge that carried the army to the continent — and the port for the timber floated down the river.

## chaoyang

On the road from the Wall into Rehe, taken in the campaign of February and March 1933.

## yanji

The seat of Jiandao, the one Manchurian province with a Korean majority, and the ground of both Korean resistance and the counter-insurgency against it.

## urumqi

The seat of Sheng Shicai, who ran Xinjiang on Soviet money until he turned to Chongqing in 1942, and the end of the road that carried Soviet aid. Never Japanese.

## kashgar

The caravan city of the southern Tarim, with a British and a Soviet consulate watching each other across it. Never Japanese.

## yining

The Ili valley at the Soviet frontier, where a Turkic rising against Chinese rule broke out in 1944.

## kangding

The old tea-and-horse gate between Sichuan and Tibet, and the seat of Xikang.

## lhasa

The seat of the Tibetan government, which stayed neutral and refused passage to an Allied supply route into China.

## shigatse

The seat of the Panchen Lama at Tashilhunpo, and the second city of Tibet.

# Korea, Taiwan, Japan and Karafuto

> What a place made, what happened in it, and when the bombers came.

## taegu

The provincial seat of North Kyŏngsang, and the apple and textile town of the south-east. The March First Movement reached it on 8 March 1919 and was put down by troops.

## kwangju

Where the student movement of November 1929 began, after Japanese schoolboys harassed Korean girls on the Naju train; it spread to some two hundred schools across the colony.

## taejon

The junction where the Seoul–Pusan trunk line meets the Honam line down to the rice country of the south-west.

## wonsan

Opened to Japanese trade in 1880, and by the 1930s the east-coast port for the fisheries and for the oil refinery built beside it.

## chongjin

Steel and a deep-water harbour built to carry Manchurian ore and soya to Japan. Soviet marines landed here on 13 August 1945.

## hamhung

Beside it at Hŭngnam stood Noguchi's Chōsen Chisso works, the largest chemical plant in the empire — fertiliser, and later explosives, on power from the Pujŏn river dams.

## sinuiju

The Korean end of the Yalu bridge to Dandong, and downstream of the Suiho dam, which was the largest in Asia when it was finished in 1941.

## kaesong

The Koryŏ capital, and the ginseng town. It was in Keiki-dō in the colonial period, which is where this map draws it, not in Hwanghae.

## nampo

Pyongyang's port, and the smelter that took Korean and Manchurian ore.

## mokpo

The cotton and rice port of the south-west, through which the Honam harvest left for Japan.

## najin

Built from a fishing village in the 1930s as the terminus of the short sea route from Manchuria to Japan, bypassing the long haul round Korea.

## tainan

The old capital of the island under the Dutch and the Qing, and the seat of the south until Japanese rule moved the centre to Taihoku.

## taichung

The centre of the rice plain, and the prefecture in which the Musha rising broke out in October 1930 — the last armed resistance by Taiwan's indigenous peoples, put down with aircraft and poison gas.

## keelung

The northern coaling port and the ferry terminus for Japan. American carrier raids struck its harbour in October 1944.

## hualien

The port of the east coast, cut off from the rest of the island by the central range and settled late.

## hsinchu

Natural gas and an air base. American bombers from China hit its airfields on 25 November 1943, the first raid of the war on Taiwan.

## chiayi

The foot of the Alishan forest railway, built by the colonial government to bring the cypress down off the mountain.

## makung

The naval anchorage in the Pescadores, which Japan took in March 1895 — a month before the treaty that gave it Taiwan.

## fukuoka

The city of the northern Kyūshū coalfield and the port for Korea. Firebombed on 19 June 1945.

## sendai

The garrison and university city of the north-east. Firebombed on 10 July 1945.

## niigata

The Japan Sea port for Korea and Manchuria. It was kept on the atomic target list and so was left largely unbombed.

## kanazawa

A castle town of the Maeda, and one of the largest cities in Japan the bombing never reached.

## kumamoto

The Sixth Division's garrison town, and the seat of the Kyūshū command. Firebombed on 1 July 1945.

## okayama

On the line to Shikoku and Kyūshū. Firebombed on 29 June 1945.

## matsuyama

A castle town and the largest city of northern Shikoku. Firebombed on 26 July 1945.

## kure

The navy yard that built the <em>Yamato</em>. Carrier raids in July 1945 sank what was left of the fleet at its moorings.

## yokosuka

The oldest of the navy yards, built with French help from 1865, and the base that guarded the approaches to Tokyo Bay.

## sasebo

The naval base facing Korea and China, from which the fleet sailed in 1894 and again in 1904.

## maizuru

The Japan Sea naval base, and after the surrender the longest-serving of the repatriation ports: some 660,000 people came home through it over thirteen years.

## aomori

The ferry crossing to Hokkaidō, and the bottleneck of the northern railway. Firebombed on 28 July 1945.

## toyohara

The capital of Karafuto, laid out on a grid by the colonial government. Soviet troops took it in August 1945 and it is Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk now.

# Southeast Asia, the Indies, India and the Pacific

> When a place was taken, and what it was taken for.

## haiphong

Tonkin's port, and where Japanese troops came ashore in September 1940 to close the rail route carrying supplies to Chongqing.

## hue

The seat of the Nguyễn emperors, where Bảo Đại reigned under French protection and declared independence under Japanese auspices in March 1945.

## phnompenh

The Cambodian royal capital under a French résident supérieur. Japan swept the French administration away here on 9 March 1945.

## vientiane

The French administrative seat in Laos, on the Mekong. The country west of the river went to Thailand in 1941.

## luangprabang

The Lao royal capital, and the one part of Laos with a king of its own under the protectorate.

## chiangmai

The seat of the old Lanna kingdom and the northern capital of Siam, from which Thai troops moved into the Shan states in May 1942.

## mandalay

The last royal capital of Burma, taken on 1 May 1942 as the army fell back to India, and retaken on 20 March 1945.

## moulmein

Fell on 31 January 1942. Thanbyuzayat, the western railhead of the Burma–Siam railway, lies just south of it.

## myitkyina

The northern railhead and airfield, taken in May 1942 and retaken in August 1944 after a siege that opened the road to China.

## kualalumpur

The capital of the Federated Malay States, taken on 11 January 1942 as the campaign came down the west coast.

## penang

Bombed on 11 December 1941 and abandoned on the 19th; the European population was evacuated and the rest was not, which was remembered.

## ipoh

The tin town of the Kinta valley, taken on 28 December 1941.

## johore

Reached on 31 January 1942, the day the causeway was blown, and the ground from which the assault on Singapore was mounted a week later.

## malacca

The oldest European settlement in Malaya, Portuguese then Dutch then British, taken on 15 January 1942.

## kuching

The Brooke capital of Sarawak, taken on 24 December 1941 with the airfield beside it.

## jesselton

The North Borneo Company's seat, taken on 9 January 1942. The rising here in October 1943 was crushed with mass executions.

## brunei

The sultan's town, taken on 6 January 1942 for the oilfield at Seria and the refinery at Lutong.

## bandung

The Dutch army's headquarters in the hills above Batavia. The surrender of the Indies was signed at Kalijati north of it on 8 March 1942.

## semarang

The port of central Java, taken in March 1942.

## soerakarta

The other princely land of central Java, ruled by its susuhunan under Dutch oversight.

## buitenzorg

The governor-general's seat in the hills, and its botanical garden, which was the scientific centre of the colony.

## cheribon

A north-coast sugar port on the Java trunk railway.

## malang

The hill town of east Java, and a Dutch garrison and air base.

## medan

The town the Sumatran plantation belt was run from — tobacco, rubber and palm oil, worked by indentured Javanese and Chinese labour.

## palembang

The refineries here were the richest prize of the southern advance, taken by parachute assault on 14 February 1942 before they could be destroyed.

## padang

The west Sumatran port, and the way out for those who got away in 1942.

## balikpapan

The Borneo oil port, taken on 24 January 1942; a Dutch demolition party fired the wells and its members were killed for it.

## pontianak

The west Borneo port, where the Japanese navy executed several thousand people in the Mandor killings of 1943 and 1944.

## bandjermasin

The south Borneo port, taken on 10 February 1942.

## makassar

The port of Celebes and the base of the Dutch eastern squadron, taken on 9 February 1942.

## manado

Taken by parachute assault on 11 January 1942, the first Japanese airborne operation of the war.

## ambon

Taken on 3 February 1942. More than three hundred Australian and Dutch prisoners were killed at Laha airfield in the days that followed.

## cebu

The second city of the islands, taken in April 1942 and a centre of the guerrilla resistance afterwards.

## davao

Taken on 20 December 1941, and before the war the largest Japanese settlement overseas — some twenty thousand people growing abaca.

## iloilo

The sugar port of Panay, taken in April 1942.

## baguio

The American summer capital in the mountains, taken on 27 December 1941.

## dili

Australian and Dutch troops landed here on 17 December 1941 to forestall Japan, in a neutral Portuguese colony; the Japanese came on 19 February 1942.

## khabarovsk

The seat of the Soviet Far Eastern command, and of the armies that stood against the Kwantung Army for a decade and crossed the border in August 1945.

## ulanbator

Urga until 1924, and the capital of a republic that only the Soviet Union recognised.

## honolulu

The territorial capital, where a third of the population was of Japanese descent; martial law was declared here on 7 December 1941 and lasted almost three years.

## agana

Guam's capital, taken on 10 December 1941 and renamed Akashi. American shelling in July 1944 left almost nothing of it standing.

## koror

The seat of the South Seas Bureau, which governed the whole mandate from Palau from 1922.

## madras

Shelled by the <em>Emden</em> in 1914, and partly evacuated in April 1942 when a Japanese landing was thought to be coming.

## karachi

The port of Sind and the airfield on the trunk route from Britain to India and beyond.

## lahore

The capital of the Punjab, and the city where the Muslim League passed the Lahore Resolution in March 1940.

## amritsar

Where troops fired on a penned crowd at Jallianwala Bagh in April 1919, killing several hundred and turning a generation against British rule.

## peshawar

The garrison city at the mouth of the Khyber, and the base for the frontier campaigns of the 1930s.

## quetta

The Baluchistan garrison and staff college, destroyed by the earthquake of 1935 in which some thirty thousand died.

## lucknow

The seat of the United Provinces, and a centre of the Congress and of the Muslim League alike.

## cawnpore

The cotton and leather mills that clothed and shod the Indian Army.

## agra

On the trunk road and railway of the Gangetic plain.

## benares

The pilgrimage city on the Ganges, and one of the princely states drawn inside the United Provinces on this map.

## jaipur

The seat of one of the larger Rajputana states, whose ruler kept his throne under treaty with the Crown.

## ahmedabad

The mill city of Gujarat, and where Gandhi's Sabarmati ashram stood; the Salt March set out from it in March 1930.

## nagpur

The seat of the Central Provinces, and the junction of the north–south and east–west trunk lines.

## poona

The Deccan garrison town. Gandhi was interned in the Aga Khan's palace here from August 1942, and his wife died in it.

## bangalore

A cantonment and, by the war, the site of India's first aircraft factory.

## mysorecity

The seat of one of the largest and best-run princely states, whose ruler kept his throne under treaty with the Crown.

## cochin

The old spice port of the Malabar coast, with a Portuguese, then Dutch, then British history behind it.

## trivandrum

The seat of Travancore, the princely state of the far south-west.

## vizag

The east-coast port, shelled from the sea by a Japanese submarine in April 1942.

## dacca

The old Mughal capital of Bengal and the jute city of the east, which the famine of 1943 struck hard.

## colombo

Raided by carrier aircraft on Easter Sunday, 5 April 1942, in the operation that drove the Eastern Fleet out of the Indian Ocean.

## calcutta

The second city of the empire and the base of the Burma front. Japanese
aircraft bombed the docks in December 1942, and the Bengal famine of 1943
killed some three million people in the country behind it while the city
itself was fed.

## shijiazhuang

The junction where the Beijing–Hankou trunk line meets the railway west into
Shanxi, which is the whole reason for the place: it was a village until the
lines came. Fell in October 1937, a fortnight after Baoding.

## tanggu

Tianjin's port at the mouth of the Hai. The truce signed here in May 1933 ended
the fighting after Rehe and gave north China a demilitarised zone, which Japan
spent the next four years pushing into.
