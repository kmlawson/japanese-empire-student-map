# Sources

Most of the coastlines and national outlines are originally Natural Earth, except China's, which is derived from the Republican province sheet below and nothing else; China's provinces are the real ones of 1928–45; Korea is the National Institute of Korean History's thirteen colonial provinces rather than modern units; the princely states of India were georeferenced by hand from a 1931 atlas; and the occupied zone and the line of control are traced from period maps and are approximations, as they say on the map itself. {.lede}

## Boundaries and coastlines

Public domain — Natural Earth's terms state the data may be used in any manner, including modifying the content and design, and that no permission is needed.<br>[github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector)
* **ENP-China — Chinese provincial boundaries, 1928–1945.** Now only used for the coastline of China. Originally the provinces of China were used from this dataset but on further investigation, the version we had seems to show interior provinces (such as Shaanxi frontier along the Yellow River) significantly distant from where it should be. I decided to keep the coastlines from this layer for convenience and then redo the provinces from historical maps (see below). Christian Henriot (June 16, 2021). * **中華民國新地圖 交通及物產詳記** (1936 [earlier version 1928]) - [HKUST Library](https://lbezone.hkust.edu.hk/bib/b933765) . This map was also consulted in tweaking the republican period provinces.
* **中華民國新地圖** (1947) - [Academia Sinica](https://gis.sinica.edu.tw/showwmts/index.php?s=ccts&l=China_Map_1947). This map was also consulted in tweaking the republican period provinces.
* **geoBoundaries ADM1 and ADM2.** The provinces ceded to Thailand in 1941, the northern Malay states, Burma by division, Kengtung by district, the Indian, Japanese and Philippine first-level units, Siam's changwat, and Bangkok's fifty khet — which are gathered by `tools/fetch_tha_bangkok.py` into Phra Nakhon and Thonburi, two provinces until 1971 that no modern first-level unit can tell apart. CC BY 4.0.<br>[github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries](https://github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries) · Runfola, D. et al. * **Topography.** Shaded relief after [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain), `SR_HR` (1:10m, 1 arc-minute) — clipped to the map's frame and warped into each of the three projections the map offers. The sheet's flat water value is remapped to white.
* **Taiwan, 1930.** The island's coastline and the divisions of the colony. Boundaries after [《日治時期臺灣行政區域沿革》](https://data.depositar.io/dataset/rd09-10), 1926年7月郡(市)界 (Academia Sinica RCHSS, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), reprojected by the author from TWD67 to TWD97 (EPSG:3826). Ajustments made: Areas labeled "蕃地" are drawn as one unit and named for the demarcation itself — the Taiwan Government-General's demarcated "Aborigine Territory" — rather than for the people the colonial state placed inside it. The distributed 1926 sheets of 《臺灣歷史文化地圖系統》 could not produce a complete administrative map: the 郡(市)界 layer omits 澎湖廳, collapses the two eastern 廳 and all 蕃地 into a single unnamed 19,000 km² residual, lacks 高雄市 and 基隆市, and carries schematic boundaries around 高雄; the corresponding 街(庄)界 layer is more degraded still. Claude Opus 4.5 was used to diagnose these problems and to reconstruct the layer used here from the 1920 街(庄)界 sheet, which is complete (304 units, including both eastern 廳, 澎湖郡, and 蕃地 resolved to 郡 level) and carries hierarchical 州/郡市/街庄 codes. There is an undeclared coordinate reference system fault affecting the whole series — the ESRI-style .prj names TWD67 without an EPSG authority code, so PROJ resolves it to an anonymous ellipsoidal CRS and applies a null datum shift, displacing the data approximately 830 m west. The layer was reprojected to TWD97 / TM2 zone 121 (EPSG:3826) via EPSG:3828, using the EPSG-registered transformation "TWD67 / TM2 zone 121 to TWD97 / TM2 zone 121 (1)". Two defective source records were repaired (新竹郡蕃地 lacked a name, 海山郡蕃地 an ID), and the documented administrative changes between July 1920 and January 1930 were applied: 基隆街 and 高雄街 raised to 市 and 高雄郡 dissolved, with 燕巢, 楠梓 and 左營庄 reassigned to 岡山郡 and 仁武庄 to 鳳山郡 (25 December 1924, 府令第90–91號); 澎湖郡 detached from 高雄州 as 澎湖廳 (July 1926); and 新竹街 and 嘉義街 raised to 市 (20 January 1930). Units were dissolved to 郡市 level with 蕃地 aggregated per 州廳, yielding 55 郡市 and 7 蕃地 blocks totalling 36,082 km², against 36,080 km² for the distributed 1926 sheet plus 澎湖. All processing was carried out in QGIS via PyQGIS scripts written by Claude.
* **Taiwan's sugar company railways, 1929** The 762 mm plantation network — 531 lines, laid by the sugar companies to bring cane to their mills and carrying passengers on some stretches as a sideline. Supplied as a shapefile in TWD97 / TM2 zone 121 (EPSG:3826) and reprojected here; `tools/build_tw_sugar.py` does the arithmetic and keeps 95% of the source's vertices, the rest being points that coincide once rounded to the map's own precision. **The provenance of this layer is still to be recorded here.**
* **Korean population, 1930** The census: 朝鮮總督府『昭和五年 朝鮮國勢調査報告』. Four tables are used — the age distribution and sex ratio of the provinces and of the fourteen 府 (pp. 10–11), household register and nationality by province (Table 19) and by city (Table 20), and occupation in nine categories, again by province and by city (Table 40). Every figure was checked against its own totals before it was used: the thirteen provinces sum to the all-Korea row exactly, the registers and the nationalities sum to each population, and the gainfully occupied plus those with no occupation sum to it as well. The one inconsistency in the source is reproduced rather than corrected — North Ch'ungch'ŏng's male occupational categories sum to 285,463 against a printed 285,457 — and is noted where it appears. The transcriptions themselves are in `data/population/sources/`.
* **Korean population, 1942** For 1942, 朝鮮總督府『昭和十七年 朝鮮人口動態統計』附録 p24 (estimated population, 1 October 1942). The totals and the sex ratios on the December 1942 cards are from there — 24,105,906 for the peninsula and a figure for each of the thirteen provinces. The percentages check: the thirteen provincial totals sum to the all-Korea figure exactly, and every stated share is within 0.05 of the total it implies. **The density is not from that table.** It is worked out here, dividing each province's population by the area of its own polygon in this map's Korea layer, measured by spherical excess; those areas come to 219,847 km² against the 220,800 usually given for colonial Korea. The four parts in a thousand are the simplification and the smallest islands, which that layer drops, and at the printed precision they move a density by one at most. The figures themselves are in `data/population/korea-1942.csv`. Cheju is counted inside Zenranan-dō in the returns and is not broken out, so the island's card says so rather than showing a figure of its own.
* **Korea coastline and administrative boundaries** National Institute of Korean History, Historical Geographic Information Database (역사지리정보DB), "Historical Administrative Districts" layer. Compiled from the Chōsen Government-General Gazette ([朝鮮總督府官報](https://db.history.go.kr/modern/gb/level.do?itemId=gb)), the 1912 地方行政區域名稱一覽 (the 1929 edition is at [NDL](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1454398/)) and the 1917 新舊對照朝鮮全道府郡面里洞名稱一覽. ## Japanese Occupations and the line of control

* The starting point of inspiration was the line of control drawn in Andrew Gordon's map in his textbook *A Modern History of Japan*, and modifications below were then begun from there to fit the December, 1942 date.
* **The Pacific mandates** — Japan's South Seas Mandate, Australia's Territory of New Guinea and the British mandate over Nauru — are traced from the *Outline Chart of the World* map produced by the U.S. Navy Hydrographic Office. Held at Princeton University, call number G3201.A1.1927.H9 and may be viewed [online here](https://maps.princeton.edu/catalog/princeton-mp48sf61n).
* **Manchukuo** is traced from 滿洲國地圖 1935, published by the research section of the South Manchuria Railway Company (南滿洲鐡道株式會社資料課). A digital version is available online at the International Research Center for Japanese Studies (Nichibunken) library, no. 004863874 and can be viewed [here](https://lapis.nichibun.ac.jp/chizu/map_detail.php?id=004863874). 
* **The Kwantung Leased Territory** is traced by hand from the above 1935 sheet.
* **Japanese Occupation of China (Approximate) and Resistance Base Areas** - This layer is drawn from the Chinese atlas of the Sino-Japanese War 武月星主編，《中國抗日戰爭史地圖集：1931–1945》 (Wu Yuexing, ed., *Historical Atlas of China's War of Resistance against Japan, 1931–1945*) p199 for 1941-2 years. 
* **The North China Area Army's own security map, September 1942 — the alternative reading offered in Layers.** 付図第五「北支那方面軍占拠地域内治安概況（昭和十七年九月中）」, an appendix to 防衛庁防衛研修所戦史室 編『北支の治安戦＜2＞』(戦史叢書 第50巻, 東京: 朝雲新聞社, 1971), held by the National Institute for Defense Studies and readable there in full. Traced and published as [kmlawson.github.io/1942-occupation-map](https://kmlawson.github.io/1942-occupation-map/). See [this blog post on Frog in a Well](https://froginawell.net/frog/2026/08/the-points-and-lines-of-japans-north-china-occupation-1942/) for more discussion. Two of the sheet's three categories are areas and are drawn here: pacified 治安地区, and un-pacified 未治安地区. The third, semi-pacified 準治安地区, is what the sheet leaves blank, and it is left blank here, so unshaded ground inside north China is not securely controlled by anyone, according to that map.<br>[nids.mod.go.jp/military_history_search/SoshoView?kanno=050](https://www.nids.mod.go.jp/military_history_search/SoshoView?kanno=050)<br>
* **Mengchiang and Outer Mongolia** are traced from the above mentioned 支那全土並附近大地圖 (*Shina zendo narabi fukin daichizu · Ōshū gensei daichizu*), a Japanese sheet of 1940. Various Chinese language histories of Japanese occupied inner Mongolia were then consulted to find settlements that are likely to have been occupied around 1942. This was then made visually distinct from the full claimed territories for Mengjiang which includes unoccupied areas to the west of Baotou.
* **The line of control in south China** - These are pieced together from various textual sources on the extent of the occupation. The line of control is drawn approximately to include places where Japanese forces were known to be garrisoned or where "puppet" administrations were set up. These areas can definitely be improved.
* **Saharat Thai Doem** — Kengtung and the trans-Salween Shan states — follows the map by Xufanc on Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, taken district by district rather than cut off with a straight line. [File:Saharat_Thai_Doem_map.png](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saharat_Thai_Doem_map.png) shows the territory Japan actually granted rather than everything Thailand claimed in the Shan and Karenni states.

**Projection.** The map is drawn on a Pacific-centred frame running 66°E–206°E and 13°S–55°N — British India to Pearl Harbor, Sakhalin to northern Australia. Longitudes are normalised to 0–360 so the map is continuous across the antimeridian. Three projections are offered in Layers, and the map is re-projected in the browser rather than re-fetched:

* **Web Mercator** is the sheet as built and the one it opens on: shapes right, north straight up, and area wrong by sec² of the latitude — 1 at the equator, 2.0 at 45°N, 3.0 at the top of this frame. Karafuto and the Soviet Far East therefore come out two to three times the size of Java and the mandate, which is the distortion the other two exist to answer.
* **Albers equal-area conic**, on 117.5°E with standard parallels at 12.5°N and 37.5°N. A conic is the usual answer for a mid-latitude region and it is honest about area, which lets the empire be compared with what it was drawn against. What a conic is not built for is 140° of longitude: the cone constant is 0.41, so the meridians fan by about 21° at the western edge and 36° at the eastern, and the far Pacific swings round noticeably. That is the projection behaving correctly rather than a fault.
* **Lambert azimuthal equal area**, centred on 25°N 115°E. Azimuthal suits a region about as tall as it is wide, and this centre sits over the South China Sea, which puts the middle of the subject where the distortion is least: the Indies and the China coast are drawn well, and the Aleutians and the far Pacific pay for it.

Both equal-area projections are worth switching to at least once. Mercator is what a reader has seen all their life, and it makes the northern empire look larger than it was.

## Transport

**Taiwan Railways** These were primarily based on the [日治時期鐵路分布圖](https://data.depositar.io/dataset/rd15-07030) (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 Taiwan) 0703b which was reprojected to TWD97. The map seems to align quite closely with historical maps where drawn. However, this map was missing several things needed for the 1930 and 1942 maps. The Hualien to Taidong line was added for both 1930 and 1942. The Hualien 1939 port extension for the 1942 map and a missing coastal station was added for 海岸驛 at 南濱 for the 1930 map. Part of the 宜蘭 line in the north was missing, as was its branch line to 菁桐坑. The 集集線 and 明治製糖南投線 departing east out of 二水 were also added to both maps. All of these were traced from 1944 [1944-美軍地形圖-1:25,000 : AM25K_1944A](https://gis.sinica.edu.tw/showwmts/index.php?s=tileserver&l=AM25K_1944A). 東港 line and 溪州/南州—枋寮 lines in the south are kept for 1942, removed for 1930 map. There are a variety of lines found on railway maps that are lines for sugar refineries and mines but some of these carried passengers. This dataset may need to be updated to take that in to account and better represent the full network.

**Korea Railways** The Korean railways make use of the [근대 철도 DB](https://www.hisgeo.info/wiki/%EA%B7%BC%EB%8C%80_%EC%B2%A0%EB%8F%84_DB) dataset by 김종혁. 「근대 철도 DB」, 2027년 8월. The lines were filtered for those created before 1931 for the 1930 map and those created before 1943 for the 1942 map. The stations were clipped as needed for each. The dataset has not yet been closely checked with contemporary maps for accuracy. Japanese names were romanized through a combination of best-guess automated romanization and looking for entries online through Japanese Wikipedia etc. This work needs to be done more methodically from scratch for better accuracy. Hangul names were romanized for McCune-Reischauer.

**Train Tools, Taiwan 1936** The working timetable behind the Train Tools is the transcription published at [kmlawson.github.io/taiwan-1936-timetable](https://kmlawson.github.io/taiwan-1936-timetable/), taken from the February 1936 timetable of the Railway Department of the Governor-General of Taiwan (臺灣總督府交通局鐵道部), which can be read at the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/taiwan-train-times-1936). It carries 346 trains over the seven passenger lines, with 5,372 calls between them, and the track between consecutive stops. The eighteen printed tables are reproduced whole at [the timetable page](timetable/taiwan-1936.html), and a station's card links to the table for its line. That page reads in English, Chinese or Japanese at the press of a button — the title, the links, the legend and the headings, but never the tables, which are a transcription and are left as printed; the column headings are translated in English only, with the printed word kept on the cell. Station names there carry their reading on a second line, kana in Japanese and Mandarin with tone marks otherwise, from the same station table the map uses.

Three things about it are worth knowing. The timetable is February 1936, which is neither of this map's two dates; it is shown on both, and the bar says so. 153 of its 187 stations are matched to the stations on this map by name, so 34 have no square to be tapped, and 22 of those have no coordinate in the source at all — a train crosses those stretches at an even pace rather than calling. Nine stops the timetable calls at were missing from the map's station sheet altogether — eight of them consecutive on the Yilan line, which left the whole north-east corner a line with trains stopping at nothing — and have been added from the Japanese and Chinese Wikipedia articles on each station, together with five elsewhere the timetable can place. The readings in those rows are from those articles and not from the timetable's own romanisation, which gives the reading of whatever a stop was called when the romaniser met it: it has 四腳亭 as Taikōho where it reads Shikyakutei, 澳底 as Ōtei where it reads Aozoko, and 瑞芳 as Ryūtanto, which is 龍潭堵, a name that station never had. The positions from the timetable are used freely: for the 153 stations both tables already held, the two agree to within a metre. The match runs the other way too: 46 of the map's 213 Taiwanese stations are not in the 1936 train tables, having been built later or standing on a stretch the transcription does not cover, and those are not drawn while the tools are open — a square on the coloured network that opens a card with no trains in it reads as a station where nothing ever called. Where two placeable stations have only unplaceable ones between them the track is drawn straight, which is done for two short joins and refused for two others that would have run 123 km and 96 km across the central mountains where no railway ran.

The description on each line's card is compiled from the Japanese and Chinese Wikipedia articles on that line — [縦貫線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B8%A6%E8%B2%AB%E7%B7%9A_(%E5%8F%B0%E6%B9%BE%E9%89%84%E8%B7%AF%E7%AE%A1%E7%90%86%E5%B1%80)), [台中線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B0%E4%B8%AD%E7%B7%9A), [海岸線](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B5%B7%E5%B2%B8%E7%B7%9A_(%E8%87%BA%E9%90%B5)), [淡水線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B7%A1%E6%B0%B4%E7%B7%9A_(%E5%8F%B0%E6%B9%BE%E9%89%84%E8%B7%AF%E7%AE%A1%E7%90%86%E5%B1%80)), [宜蘭線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AE%9C%E8%98%AD%E7%B7%9A), [平渓線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B9%B3%E6%BA%AA%E7%B7%9A), [集集線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9B%86%E9%9B%86%E7%B7%9A), [屏東線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B1%8F%E6%9D%B1%E7%B7%9A) and [台東線](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B0%E6%9D%B1%E7%B7%9A) — for the construction dates, the section openings and the gauges, and has not been checked against a printed history. The journey times in those descriptions are not from there: they are measured from this transcription, so the reader can check each one against the printed table a link away. The two kinds of statement are kept apart in the wording — "in this table" and "a through working" are the measured ones.

The colours are the source's own, one per line. Where one of them cannot be told apart from the land it is drawn over — the trunk line's red and this map's colonial red are the same red — it is darkened or lightened until it can be, and the legend, the trains and the swatches in a station's card all show the shade actually drawn.

## Software

Georeferencing of various historical maps done with QGIS. Website code created with Anthropic's Claude, with Konrad Lawson at the prompt. No third-party JavaScript libraries: the page is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and the build scripts use only the Python standard library.
