# The coordinator's share of the South-East Asia pass: the territories and the Philippines

16 September 2026. The same four questions as the agents' passes. Read by
hand; links checked against the Wikipedia API in batches (the site was
answering 429 to this machine while four of us fetched at once, so the
batches were spaced and retried).

## Territories: 46 records (1930: 22 ids, 1942: 24) in `texts/territories/`

* **Hover vs card.** No `short` column; the hover shows the `en` gloss after
  the em dash, and every gloss is under 88 characters. Nothing moved.
* **Links.** All 35 distinct URLs resolve, none a disambiguation, none a
  redirect. None changed: each is the right subject for the record.
* **Style.** Three rewrites — `philippines` 1930 "a brutal war" → "a war";
  `dei` 1942 "The object of the whole southern advance." → "The main
  objective of the southern advance, for its oil."; `miangas` 1930 lost
  "and one of the most cited decisions in international law".
* **Facts.** Every date and figure in the 46 notes was read against what I
  know and the uncertain ones fetched:
  * `cocos` 1942: "Airfields were built in 1944 for the bombing of Java and
    Singapore" — the article gives no date for the West Island airstrip;
    sentence removed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_(Keeling)_Islands).
  * `spratly` 1942: the note attached "both groups" to Takao as the Shinnan
    Guntō. The Shinnan Guntō was the Spratlys; the article says the Paracels
    were placed "under the administration of either Taiwan or Hainan".
    Rewritten: the Spratlys were attached, the Paracels garrisoned as well
    (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands).
  * `borneo_br` 1942 "British surrender 1 April 1942" confirmed: "After ten
    weeks in the jungle-covered mountains, Allied troops surrendered on
    1 April" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_British_Borneo).
  * Checked and left: the 1923 Nepal treaty, Tumlong 1861 and the Calcutta
    Convention of 17 March 1890, Punakha 1910, the Singapore base begun 1923,
    Seria 1929, the 8 March 1942 Dutch surrender, the 14 October 1943
    republic, the 20 August 1943 transfers, Si Rat Malai that October,
    the Emden 1914, the Cocos mutiny of May 1942, Palmas 4 April 1928, the
    Turtle Islands convention of 2 January 1930 and transfer of 16 October
    1947, the Franco-Thai settlement of 9 May 1941.

## Philippines: `philippines.csv/.md` (43) and `philippine-islands.csv/.md` (11)

* **Hover vs card.** Every row has a short and a note. Five shorts were the
  note's opening cut mid-list and read as fragments — `Bulacan` ("…flat"),
  `NuevaEcija` ("…a wide, flat"), `Lanao` ("…cool"), `Albay` (an em-dash
  clause), `Manila` ("the reason for everything else") — rewritten as phrases.
* **Links.** All 54 resolve and name the province or island. None changed.
* **Style.** Twelve passages in `philippines.md` rewritten to plain fact: the
  "reputation for being difficult" sentence on Batangas, "the reason for
  everything else" on Manila, "you can cross it in an hour", "one of several
  such contracts that later looked different than they had", superlatives
  that no source would settle ("the poorest province on the island", "one
  of the poorest and least visited", "the largest Marian devotion in the
  country", "the most agrarian-conflicted in the country"), and "long and
  bloody fighting".
* **Facts.**
  * `NuevaEcija`: "the Sakdalista rising of May 1935 took town halls here" —
    the rising was in Laguna, Bulacan, Rizal and Cavite, not Nueva Ecija.
    Removed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakdalista).
  * `NegrosOriental`: Silliman "the first American institution of higher
    learning in Asia" → "the first Protestant institution of higher
    education in the Philippines", which is what the article says
    (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silliman_University).
  * `Zambales`: "The Coto mine at Masinloc, opened in 1935, sat on one of the
    largest refractory chromite deposits in the world" — neither the year nor
    the superlative could be sourced; now "worked a large refractory chromite
    deposit".
  * Left as they stand, being what the standard accounts say: Mayon's
    eruptions of 1897, 1928 and 1938; the Bataan surrender of 9 April and
    the 60–80,000 marched; the Batanes landing of 8 December 1941; Del Monte
    at Camp Phillips from 1926; the Malolos congress; the tobacco monopoly
    of 1782–1882; Dewey on 1 May 1898; Ohta Kyozaburo from 1907 and the
    twenty thousand Japanese of Davao; the Basi Revolt of 1807; Los Baños
    from 1909; La Union from 1850; Lanao 1902–13; Manila's 26 December open
    city, 2 January occupation and the hundred thousand dead of February
    1945; the 1929 division of Misamis; Benguet gold from 1903 and Baguio
    from 1904; Bud Dajo 1906 and Bud Bagsak 1913; the Dagupan railway of
    1892 and the Lingayen landings of 22 December 1941 and 9 January 1945;
    Goodyear on Basilan from 1928; Puerto Princesa, December 1944.
