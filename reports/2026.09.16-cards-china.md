## Mechanism check against map.js

`splitGloss` (map.js:680), `briefOf` (945, `BRIEF_MAX` 120), `hoverShort`
(967), `shortOf` (984, `SHORT_MAX` 88) and the card builder (10533,
`head.note || split.gloss || shortOf(head)`) all behave as the brief
describes. Two details worth recording:

* `shortOf` falls back to the `en` gloss **only when it is ≤ 88 characters**, so
  a province paragraph in `en` can never reach the hover. The 27 provinces that
  carry one also carry a `short`, so the hover was in fact working; what was
  wrong was the shape, not the output. Xinjiang and Xizang had neither, and so
  had no hover line at all.
* `splitGloss` capitalises the gloss and adds a full stop unless it has one, and
  the card takes `head.note` first. Moving the paragraph from `en` to
  `china.md` therefore changes nothing a reader sees except that the card's
  first line now comes from the note.

### Not changed, and why

* **`mandalay`'s `**147,932**`** looked like markdown shipped raw. It is not:
  `map.js` line 10295 has its own renderer (`EMPH`) that turns `*…*` into `<em>`
  and `**…**` into `<strong>`, built from text nodes, plus a narrow
  `[text](url)` rule. So the asterisks are the author's emphasis and reach the
  card as emphasis. Worth knowing before anyone else "fixes" one — the brief's
  note that `<em>` is the way to emphasise is only half of it.
* **`anyang` is glossed `Ānyáng (Changteh)`, and so is `changde`.** Not an
  error: Anyang was Zhangde fu, 彰德, and Changde is 常德, and both come out
  "Changteh" in postal romanisation. Two markers on one map with the same
  second name, both correct.
* **`jinan` (the site) links to the city, not the [Jinan incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_incident)**,
  although its note is entirely about May 1928. The record is a city marker
  (`cat: city`) so the city article is what it means.
* `imphalcity`, `haikou`, `hongkong`, `kengtung`, `punakha`, `kathmandu`,
  `zhanjiang`, `macaucity` and `benares` all say in their notes that the map
  already draws the thing at a larger scale. That reads oddly out of context
  but it is a deliberate convention across the gazetteer.
* Long notes I left long, because they are the author's and the instruction is
  to move prose rather than cut it: `sites/nanjing` (315 characters),
  `sites/yanan` (326), `sites/huayuankou` (371 before the edit),
  `city-names/mandalay` (338).
