# Fact-check of the interactive Japanese Empire map

**Date:** 17 August 2026
**Scope:** `data.js`, `cities-gaz.js` (and its sources `data/cities-1930.csv`, `data/cities-1942.csv`), `index.html`, `sources.html`, `SOURCES.md`, `README.md`.
**Method:** every record in `data.js` read in full; `cities-gaz.js` audited programmatically by polity and by name; `tasks.md` and `tasks-done.md` read first, so that decisions recorded there as deliberate are not reported as errors. Nothing in the repository was modified except this file.

Two general remarks before the list. First, the content is in very good order: the great majority of dates, sequences and administrative statuses check out, and the map is unusually careful about anachronism. Most of what follows is either (a) drift between prose written for an earlier version of the geometry and the geometry as it now stands, or (b) the new city gazetteer, which has not been held to the standard the rest of the map is held to. Second, several items below are places where the map contradicts *itself*; those are worth fixing first, because a student who notices one has been given two answers.

---

## A. CLEAR ERRORS

### A1. The Ryukyu group is labelled "Okinawa Prefecture", but half of the islands in it were Kagoshima Prefecture

**Location:** `data.js:1993` (comment) and the sub-unit block that follows, `data.js:1994–2005`; the parent record `data.js:126–130` and `data.js:501–505` (`id: 'ryukyu'`).

**Currently says:** the comment reads `/* the Ryukyus, north to south: Okinawa Prefecture, annexed in 1879 */`, and the territory those islands answer to is `en: 'Ryūkyū Islands / Okinawa Prefecture'`, `ja: '沖縄県 (Okinawa-ken)'`, `when: 'Annexed 1879'`.

**What is wrong:** the sub-unit list runs `'Yakushima'`, `'Kuchinoerabujima'`, `'Kuchinoshima'`, `'Nakanoshima'`, `'Tairajima'`, `'Suwanosejima'`, `'Akusekijima'`, `'Kikaijima'`, `'Amami Ōshima'`, `'Tokunoshima'`, `'Okinoerabujima'`, `'Yoronjima'` before it reaches Iheya and Okinawa. None of those twelve was in Okinawa Prefecture on either of the map's dates. The Ōsumi islands (Yakushima, Kuchinoerabu), the Tokara islands and the whole Amami group were **Kagoshima Prefecture** — Amami as Ōshima-gun (大島郡), administered from Kagoshima from 1879 onward. Yakushima and Tanegashima are not usually reckoned part of the Ryūkyūs at all. So a student hovering Amami Ōshima is told it is Okinawa Prefecture, annexed in 1879, which is false in both halves.

**Should say:** either split the northern islands into their own record (Kagoshima Prefecture, Ōshima-gun, with a line saying the old Ryūkyū Kingdom's northern islands were taken by Satsuma in 1609 and stayed with Kagoshima after 1879), or, if the geometry cannot be split, retitle the parent as "Ryūkyū and Ōsumi Islands — Okinawa and Kagoshima Prefectures" and drop "Okinawa Prefecture" from the individual northern islands' context. The comment at 1993 should be corrected either way.

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** any prefectural handbook of the period; standard: Amami was Kagoshima-ken Ōshima-gun from 1879 to 1946 (US administration 1946–53), Okinawa Prefecture was created out of the Ryūkyū Domain in 1879 and ran from Iheya/Okinawa south.

### A2. "Haterumajima — the southernmost of the empire"

**Location:** `data.js:2016`.

**Currently says:** `'Haterumajima': { en: 'Haterumajima — the southernmost of the empire', zh: '波照間島' }`.

**What is wrong:** Hateruma is at 24.05° N. On the 1930 map the empire already reached Eluanbi in Taiwan (21.9° N) and, through the South Seas Mandate, the equator; on the 1942 map it reached Timor and the Solomons. Hateruma is the southernmost *inhabited island of Japan today*, and the southernmost point of Okinawa Prefecture — not of the empire.

**Should say:** "the southernmost inhabited island of Japan proper" or "the southernmost of Okinawa Prefecture".

**Confidence:** high.

### A3. Chengteh is dated to February 1933; the city fell on 4 March

**Location:** `data.js:1032–1036`, `id: 'chengde'`, `date: 'Occupied February 1933'`. Compare the same record's own note, "Taken in a ten-day campaign", and `data.js:1900–1901` (`'Je Hol'`: "A province of the Republic until February 1933").

**What is wrong:** Operation Nekka opened on 23 February 1933; Chengteh itself was entered by the Kwantung Army on **4 March 1933**. "Occupied February 1933" is right for the campaign and wrong for the city, and it sits oddly beside "a ten-day campaign" in the same card.

**Should say:** `date: 'Occupied 4 March 1933'`. The Jehol *province* records ("Japan took it in February 1933", `data.js:192`; "until February 1933", `data.js:1901`) are defensible as the start of the invasion, but would read better as "February–March 1933".

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** standard accounts of the Jehol operation (e.g. Coox, *Nomonhan*, ch. 1; Chinese/Japanese chronologies of 熱河作戦: 承徳 entered 4 March 1933).

### A4. The Spratlys: the 1930 and 1933 acts are the wrong way round

**Location:** `data.js:395–400`, `id: 'spratly'` (1930 epoch).

**Currently says:** "Britain claimed them from 1877 and did nothing with them; France annexed them in April 1930 and occupied Spratly Island and Itu Aba in 1933, attaching them to Cochinchina."

**What is wrong:** the sequence is inverted. In **April 1930** the gunboat *Malicieuse* took possession of **Spratly Island** — an occupation of one island. The **annexation of the group** (nine islands, including Itu Aba) came in **July 1933**, published in the *Journal officiel* on 26 July 1933, and the islands were attached to Bà Rịa province of Cochinchina in December 1933. The record's own `when:` line, "Annexed by France, April 1930", carries the same slip.

**Should say:** "France occupied Spratly Island in April 1930 and annexed the group — nine islands, Itu Aba among them — in 1933, attaching it to Cochinchina." `when:` → "Occupied by France, April 1930; annexed 1933".

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** Monique Chemillier-Gendreau, *Sovereignty over the Paracel and Spratly Islands*, docs. of 1930 and 1933; RSIS, "Before the Nine-Dash Line: France's Sovereignty Claims in the South China Sea (1930–1956)".

### A5. Zuikaku was not damaged at the Coral Sea

**Location:** `data.js:1217–1221`, `id: 'coralsea'`.

**Currently says:** "…damage to Shokaku and Zuikaku kept both out of Midway a month later."

**What is wrong:** *Shōkaku* took three bomb hits and went home for repair. *Zuikaku* was **undamaged**; she missed Midway because her air group had been gutted — she lost some 23 aircraft and their crews and could not be reconstituted in time. Saying both were damaged loses the point that carrier aircrew, not hulls, were the binding constraint.

**Should say:** "…*Shōkaku* was bombed and *Zuikaku*'s air group destroyed, and both missed Midway a month later."

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** Parshall & Tully, *Shattered Sword*, ch. 2; Lundstrom, *The First Team*.

*Also in this record and its neighbours:* ship names are set without macrons (Shoho, Shokaku, Zuikaku) while people are not (Tōgō, Tōjō, Saigō Takamori). Worth normalising to Shōhō / Shōkaku.

### A6. Tulagi: the landings were 7 August, not 8–9 August

**Location:** `data.js:731–735`, `id: 'tulagi'`.

**Currently says:** `when: 'Taken by the Americans 8–9 August 1942'`; note: "retaken by the Marines on 8 August, with Gavutu and Tanambogo falling the next day."

**What is wrong:** Watchtower's landings on Tulagi, Gavutu and Tanambogo were all on **7 August 1942**, the same day as Guadalcanal (which the adjacent record dates correctly to 7 August). Tulagi was declared secure on **8 August**; Gavutu was taken on the evening of 7 August and Tanambogo on 8 August. As written the map has Tulagi assaulted a day after Guadalcanal and Tanambogo falling on the 9th.

**Should say:** `when: 'Taken by the Americans 7–8 August 1942'`; "…assaulted on 7 August, the same morning as Guadalcanal, and secured on the 8th, with Gavutu and Tanambogo taken alongside it."

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** Frank, *Guadalcanal*, ch. 3; USMC monograph *The Guadalcanal Campaign*.

### A7. Howland and Baker were bombed from the air, not shelled by a warship

**Location:** `data.js:690–694`, `id: 'uspacific'` (1942).

**Currently says:** "A Japanese warship shelled Howland and Baker on 8 December 1941, the day of the Pearl Harbor attack in this hemisphere, and the young Hawaiian colonists were taken off in February."

**What is wrong:** two things. (i) The attack of 8 December 1941 was by **fourteen land-based G3M bombers of the Chitose Kōkūtai flying from Kwajalein**, which killed two of the Kamehameha School colonists on Howland (Richard Whaley and Joseph Kealoha). A submarine shelled the wrecked buildings later. (ii) The survivors were evacuated on **31 January 1942** (USS *Helene*), not in February. (iii) "the day of the Pearl Harbor attack in this hemisphere" is confusing at best: Howland keeps UTC−12, so the moment of the Pearl Harbor attack was still 7 December there; the raid was the following day.

**Should say:** "Japanese bombers from Kwajalein struck Howland and Baker on 8 December 1941, the day after Pearl Harbor, killing two of the young Hawaiian colonists; the survivors were taken off at the end of January."

**Confidence:** high on (i) and (ii).
**Source:** US Fish & Wildlife / Office of Insular Affairs histories of the Howland–Baker–Jarvis colonisation scheme; standard accounts of the Kamehameha School colonists.

### A8. The 1930 gazetteer calls Singapore "Shōnantō" and Changchun "Hsinking"

**Location:** `data/cities-1930.csv` lines 21 and 114 → `cities-gaz.js`, `JMAP.GAZ.e1930`:
`{"id":"singapore","n":"Singapore (Shōnantō)",…}` and `{"id":"changchun","n":"Chángchūn (Hsinking)",…,"p":"Republic of China"}`.

**What is wrong:** both names are 1932/1942 names on the 1930 map. Changchun became Hsinking in March 1932; Singapore became Shōnantō on 16 February 1942. `JMAP.EPOCH_OVERRIDES` in `data.js` gets both right (`singapore: { e1930: { en: 'Singapore' … } }`, `changchun: { e1930: { en: 'Changchun', ja: '長春 (Chōshun)' … } }`), and `SOURCES.md:317–319` states the policy explicitly: "so the 1930 map does not call Singapore Syonan-to or describe Changchun as the capital of Manchukuo." The gazetteer layer, which is what now labels cities, breaks that policy in the two places the policy names.

**Should say:** 1930 rows: `Singapore`, `Chángchūn`.

**Confidence:** high (the map's own documentation is the authority).

### A9. The 1942 gazetteer puts Chengchow and Wenchow inside the occupation and Hofei outside it — all three wrong, two of them contradicting the About panel

**Location:** `data/cities-1942.csv` lines 129, 123, 224 → `cities-gaz.js`, `JMAP.GAZ.e1942`.

- `zhengzhou, Zhèngzhōu (Chengchow), … Occupied China`
- `wenzhou, Wēnzhōu (Wenchow), … Occupied China`
- `hefei, Héféi (Hofei), … Free China`

**What is wrong:**
- **Chengchow**: `index.html:174–176` says "so Loyang and Chengchow are outside it, both holding until 1944". Chengchow was raided and briefly entered in October 1941 and evacuated; it fell for good in the Ichi-Gō operation of April 1944. In December 1942 it was Chinese-held. The gazetteer contradicts the About panel and the traced zone.
- **Wenchow**: `index.html:176–177` says the front runs "through Chekiang taking in Kinhwa but not Chuchow or Wenchow". Wenchow was occupied briefly in April–May 1941 and again in July–August 1942, and abandoned each time; it was held permanently only from September 1944. In December 1942 it was Chinese-held. Again the gazetteer contradicts the About panel.
- **Hofei**: Japanese forces took Hefei on **14 May 1938** and held it to the surrender; the Anhwei provincial government moved to Lihuang (Chin-chai). It cannot be Free China in December 1942. (The Chinese pocket the About panel describes is in *north-west* Anhwei and the Dabie Shan, which is not Hefei.)

**Should say:** 1942 rows: Chengchow → `Free China`; Wenchow → `Free China`; Hofei → `Occupied China`.

**Confidence:** high for Hofei and Chengchow; high-medium for Wenchow (the point is that it was not held on the map's date).
**Source:** Hsu Long-hsuen & Chang Ming-kai, *History of the Sino-Japanese War*; 中国抗日战争正面战场作战记 for the Chekiang-Kiangsi campaign of 1942; Chinese municipal chronologies for 合肥沦陷, 14 May 1938.

*Worth a second look while you are there:* `beihai, Pakhoi (Beihai), … Occupied China`. Japan landed at Pakhoi in November 1939 in the Nanning operation and pulled back from that theatre through 1940; Nanning is (correctly) Free China in the same file. Pakhoi's December 1942 status should be checked rather than assumed.

### A10. "Straits Settlements" is used as a December 1942 polity

**Location:** `data/cities-1942.csv` lines 19, 100, 257, 417 → `cities-gaz.js` 1942: `singapore` (`"of":"Straits Settlements","p":"Straits Settlements"`), `penang`, `malacca`, `labuan`.

**What is wrong:** the Straits Settlements did not exist as a going administration in December 1942. Singapore was Shōnan-tokubetsushi under the 25th Army's military administration; Penang and Malacca were under the Malaya Military Administration; Labuan was Maeda-shima under the British Borneo military administration. `data.js` itself says so — `PROVINCE_EPOCH.e1942` gives `'Labuan': { en: 'Labuan — under the Japanese military administration of British Borneo' }` — and the `borneo_br` note spells out the renaming. The gazetteer contradicts both.

**Should say:** 1942 rows: Singapore → `Shōnantō (Japanese military administration)`; Penang, Malacca → `Malaya (Japanese-occupied)`, which the file already uses for the four northern states; Labuan → `British Borneo (Japanese-occupied)`.

**Confidence:** high.

### A11. The About panel's Manchukuo and Mengchiang sentences describe geometry the map no longer uses

**Location:** `index.html:166–168` and `index.html:184–187`.

**Currently says:** "Manchukuo is Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang and Jehol as they actually stood, and Mengchiang is Chahar and Suiyuan"; and "Manchukuo redrew the three north-eastern provinces into nineteen of its own between 1934 and 1941; they are drawn here as Fengtien, Kirin and Heilungkiang, the provinces Manchukuo was made out of."

**What is wrong:** both statements were superseded. Manchukuo is now a traced outline from 滿洲國地圖 1935 with **its own fourteen provinces** (`data.js:1871–1901`; `SOURCES.md:236`; `sources.html:223–234`; `README.md:145–147` all record the change), and Mengchiang is now its own traced boundary rather than two Chinese provinces (`data.js:562–571`). The About panel is the only reader-facing text still describing the old arrangement, and it tells the reader the opposite of what the map now does.

**Should say:** rewrite to: Manchukuo is drawn from the South Manchuria Railway's own 1935 sheet, with the provinces of that sheet; Mengchiang from a traced boundary of the regime rather than from whole Chinese provinces. See also B7 below on the date of that provincial scheme.

**Confidence:** high (verified against the current data).

### A12. `sources.html` calls the 1930 Manchurian provinces "Fengtien"

**Location:** `sources.html:56–58`.

**Currently says:** "On the 1930 map these draw Manchuria as Fengtien, Kirin and Heilungkiang, and Jehol beside them."

**What is wrong:** the Nationalist government renamed Fengtien province **Liaoning in February 1929**; the name Fengtien came back only under Manchukuo. `data.js` gets this exactly right — `PROVINCE_EPOCH.e1930` gives `'Liaoning': { en: 'Liáoníng (Fengtien until 1929)' }` and `e1942` gives `'Liaoning': { en: 'Fèngtiān (Fengtien)' }` — and `SOURCES.md:30` gets it right too. `sources.html` is alone in the error.

**Should say:** "as Liaoning, Kirin and Heilungkiang".

**Confidence:** high.

### A13. "Eleven provinces ruled directly" is a post-1937 figure on the 1930 map

**Location:** `data.js:235–237` (`id: 'princelystates'`, 1930 epoch); the identical text is repeated at `data.js:804–806` for 1942, where it is correct.

**Currently says:** "British India was a patchwork: eleven provinces ruled directly, and beside them some six hundred princely states…"

**What is wrong:** eleven governors' provinces is the arrangement created by the Government of India Act 1935 and in force from 1 April 1937 (Sind and Orissa separated; NWFP raised). In 1930 there were **nine** governors' provinces — and the NWFP was not one of them until 1932, as the map's own `PROVINCE_EPOCH.e1930` entry says ("a chief commissioner's province until 1932") — plus a handful of chief commissioners' provinces and Burma.

**Should say:** for 1930, "nine governors' provinces and a handful of smaller chief commissioners' provinces". The 1942 copy can stay as it is. (This is one of the few places where the map re-uses one note across both epochs and pays for it.)

**Confidence:** high.

### A15. The documented naming convention is the reverse of what the data does for China

**Location:** `data.js:11–15` (header comment), `index.html:156–160`, `README.md:37–39`, `SOURCES.md:296–300`. Correct in `sources.html:305–313`.

**Currently says:** "the name in English as it was used at the time, with the present-day name after it in brackets where they differ — **Mukden (Shenyang)** … **Peking (Beijing)** … **Hsinking (Changchun)**".

**What the data does:** `'Shěnyáng (Mukden)'`, `'Běijīng (Peking / Peiping)'`, `'Chángchūn (Hsinking)'`, `'Chóngqìng (Chungking)'`, `'Guǎngzhōu (Canton)'` — pinyin with tones first, period form in brackets, for every Chinese place. Non-Chinese places do follow the documented rule (`'Keijō (Seoul)'`, `'Taihoku (Taipei)'`, `'Batavia (Jakarta)'`). `tasks.md:1460–1468` records the pinyin-first switch as deliberate, and `sources.html` documents it correctly; three other files were not updated, and two of them illustrate the rule with the very examples that now run the other way.

**Should say:** add the China exception everywhere the rule is stated: "Chinese places take the present-day pinyin form first, with tone marks, and the period spelling in brackets — Shěnyáng (Mukden), Běijīng (Peking / Peiping) — because that is the form a student can look up. Everywhere else the period name leads."

**Confidence:** high.

**Related, and worth tidying at the same time:** three Chinese entries in `JMAP.BROWSE` still lead with the period form against all their neighbours — `data.js:1481` `'Mutankiang (Mudanjiang)'`, `data.js:1491` `'Antung (Dandong)'`, `data.js:1689` `'Yenki (Yanji)'` (also `cities-gaz.js` 1930 `Antung (Dandong)`, and 1942 `Chüanchow (Quanzhou)`, `Haikou (Kiungchow)`, `Tungchow (Tongzhou)`, `Samah (Sanya)`, `Pakhoi (Beihai)`, `Dolonnor (Duolun)`). And the `JMAP.BROWSE` header comment at `data.js:1434–1435` — "English names are the period ones with the modern name in brackets where they differ" — describes the Korean and Taiwanese entries but not the Chinese ones underneath it.

### A17. "The only land battle fought on Japanese home soil" contradicts the Iwo Jima record

**Location:** `data.js:968–972`, `id: 'naha'`.

**Currently says:** "…the site of the only land battle fought on Japanese home soil, in which around a quarter of Okinawan civilians died."

**What is wrong:** the map elsewhere states that the Bonins, Iwo Jima included, were **part of Tokyo Prefecture** (`data.js:131–136`, `rule: 'Japanese, part of Tokyo prefecture'`), and carries Iwo Jima as a battle. The Kuriles (Shumshu, August 1945) were Japanese too. The usual careful formulations are "the only battle of the war fought on one of the Japanese home islands' prefectures with a large civilian population", or simply "the only land battle of the war fought on Japanese soil with the civilian population in it".

**Should say:** something like "the only land battle of the war fought on Japanese soil among a large civilian population — a quarter of Okinawan civilians died in it."

**Confidence:** high that as written it contradicts the map's own Iwo Jima records.

### A18. Mengchiang: three chronological problems and an out-of-date name

**Locations:** `data.js:197–202` (1930, `id: 'chaharsuiyuan'`); `data.js:567–571` (1942, `id: 'mengjiang'`); `data.js:1064–1068` (`id: 'kalgan'`).

**Currently says:**
- 1930: "From 1936 they became the Japanese client regime of Mengchiang under Prince Demchugdongrub, **with its capital at Kalgan**."
- 1942: `when: 'Client regime from 1936–39'`; `orig`/`zh`: `蒙疆聯合自治政府`; note: "Built out of the Chinese provinces of **Chahar and Suiyuan**."
- Kalgan: `date: 'Capital of Mengchiang from 1937'`.

**What is wrong:**
1. **Kalgan in 1936.** The Mongol Military Government (蒙古軍政府) proclaimed in May 1936 sat at Hua-te / Changpei in northern Chahar. Kalgan was in Chinese hands until the Japanese took it in **August 1937**; it became the seat of the Mengchiang United Committee that November and of the United Autonomous Government from September 1939. The 1930 note therefore gives the regime a capital it did not have for another year and a half, and it disagrees with the Kalgan record's own "from 1937".
2. **North Shansi is missing.** Mengchiang was assembled from three bodies — the Mongol regime, the South Chahar Autonomous Government and the **North Shansi (Chin-pei) Autonomous Government** around Tatung. "Built out of the Chinese provinces of Chahar and Suiyuan" leaves out a third of it, and contradicts the map's own gazetteer, which files `datong, Dàtóng (Tatung)` under `"p":"Mengjiang"`.
3. **The name on the December 1942 map.** 蒙疆聯合自治政府 is an attested form for 1939–41, but the regime was renamed **蒙古自治邦** (Mōko Jichihō, the Mongolian Autonomous Federation) on 4 August 1941 and carried that name on the map's date.
4. `when: 'Client regime from 1936–39'` reads as a regime that ended in 1939; it presumably means "formed in stages, 1936–39", which is worth saying plainly.

**Should say:** 1930 — "From 1936 the Mongol prince Demchugdongrub headed a Japanese-sponsored government in northern Chahar; after Japan took Kalgan in 1937 it grew, with the Chinese autonomous governments of southern Chahar and northern Shansi, into the client regime of Mengchiang, with its capital at Kalgan from 1939." 1942 — `when: 'Client regime, assembled 1936–39'`; add 蒙古自治邦 as the name from August 1941; add northern Shansi to the note.

**Confidence:** high on (1), (2) and (3).
**Source:** Sechin Jagchid, *The Last Mongol Prince*; standard chronologies of 蒙疆聯合自治政府 → 蒙古自治邦, 4 August 1941.

### A19. Thailand got four provinces out of Indochina, not three

**Location:** `data.js:582` (`id: 'indochina'`, 1942): "let Thailand carry off three provinces in 1941"; and `data.js:774–779` (`id: 'cededthai'`): "Renamed Phra Tabong, Phibunsongkhram and Lan Chang."

**Confidence:** high.
**Source:** E. Thadeus Flood, "The 1940 Franco-Thai Border Dispute", *Journal of Southeast Asian History* 10:2 (1969); Thai administrative gazettes of 1941.

### A21. Kwangchowan's note describes stripes the map does not draw, and an Indochina that is no longer drawn that way

**Location:** `data.js:853–857`, `id: 'guangzhouwan'` (1942).

**Currently says:** "A French leased territory on the Leizhou peninsula, **run from Indochina and drawn like it: the French colour with Japanese stripes across it.**"

**What is wrong:** the record has no `hatch` key — the only hatched 1942 records are `turtle`, `saharat`, `cocos`, `timor_pt` and `guadalcanal_i` — so there are no Japanese stripes on it; and Indochina is drawn in the *occupied* colour, not "the French colour with Japanese stripes", since the reversal recorded at `tasks.md:2276–2278`. The sentence describes a design that was abandoned. (`tasks.md:1645` states the intended treatment plainly: "the leasehold is drawn French because that is whose it was".)

**Should say:** "…drawn in the French colour because that is whose it was; the dashed line of control runs round it because Japanese forces were inside it before the formal occupation of February 1943."

**Confidence:** high (verified against the record's own keys).

**Same class, code comment only:** `data.js:66–69` still says "Guadalcanal, Malaita, Papua, Portuguese Timor — keeps its owner's colour and takes red stripes across it". Malaita and Papua no longer do (`tasks-done.md` #29); only Guadalcanal, Timor and Kengtung carry that treatment.

### A22. Papua is drawn as occupied territory

**Location:** `data.js:712–717`, `id: 'newguinea_au'` (1942), `cat: 'occupied'` over the whole island, with `rule: 'Australian territory and mandate, part under Japanese occupation'`.

**What is wrong:** the whole of Papua and the mandate take the occupation colour, while the record's own note says "Port Moresby was the objective and was never reached". A student reading colour rather than text is told Japan held Port Moresby, Milne Bay and the whole Papuan south coast in December 1942, which is the opposite of the truth and of the dashed line drawn beside it. `tasks.md:1561–1563` already flags this: "**Still to settle:** the *colour* has not been changed to match. All of New Guinea is still drawn in the occupation salmon, so the line now says one thing and the fill another."

**Should say:** at minimum, hatch the island as contested the way Guadalcanal is; better, split Papua south of the range from the occupied northern coast, as the line of control already does.

**Confidence:** high that the map contradicts itself here; the remedy is a design decision.

### A23. `SOURCES.md` files Sinkiang as a polity apart from China

**Location:** `SOURCES.md:241`: "| **Tibet, Sinkiang** | The Xizang and Xinjiang polygons, which the source itself treats as polities apart from China | Good |".

**What is wrong:** the map's own records take great care to say the opposite about Sinkiang — `data.js:203–208`, "Xinjiang was formally a province of the Republic of China… **Xinjiang was not an independent state**, but it lay largely outside effective Nationalist control"; and in 1942 it is `cat: 'freechina'`, `within: 'freechina'`. Tibet is a different case and is handled separately. Grouping the two in the provenance table, and attributing the judgement to the dataset, contradicts the careful line taken in the content.

**Should say:** split the row; say for Sinkiang that the polygon is used for geometry only and that the map treats it as a province of the Republic.

**Confidence:** high.

### A24. Korean provinces in the gazetteer are given in English MR forms, against the stated policy and against the Taiwanese treatment in the same file

**Location:** `cities-gaz.js` / `data/cities-*.csv`, `capital_of` column: `South Kyŏngsang` (pusan), `South P'yŏngan` (pyongyang), `North Hamgyŏng` (chongjin), `Hwanghae` (haeju), `North Chŏlla`, `Kangwŏn`, and so on — against `Taichū Prefecture`, `Karenkō Prefecture`, `Hōko Prefecture`, `Takao Prefecture` for Taiwan in the same column.

**What is wrong:** `index.html:180–182` states the policy: "These are the units of the period and they carry their period names — **Kankyōhoku-dō rather than North Hamgyŏng**". `data.js:2190–2202` follows it exactly. The gazetteer does the reverse for Korea while following it for Taiwan, so hovering Pusan and hovering Taichū give names on two different systems.

**Should say:** `Keishōnan-dō`, `Heiannan-dō`, `Kankyōhoku-dō`, `Kōkai-dō` etc., with the MR form in brackets if you want it, matching `JMAP.PROVINCES`.

**Confidence:** high.

### A25. The South Seas Mandate is dated from 1919 in one record and 1920 in another

**Location:** `data.js:163–168` and `data.js:541–546` (`id: 'nanyo'`): `when: 'Held by Japan from 1919 (seized from Germany in 1914)'`, note "held by Japan from 1919 as a League of Nations Class C mandate"; against `data.js:287–292` (`id: 'mandate_jp'`): `when: 'Japanese Class C mandate from 1920…'`, note "was granted them as a Class C mandate by the League in **December 1920**".

**What is wrong:** not a factual error so much as two answers to one question in adjacent records. The defensible chronology is: occupied October 1914; allocated to Japan by the Supreme Council in May 1919 and confirmed by the Treaty of Versailles; the mandate itself conferred by the League Council on 17 December 1920; civil administration from 1922.

**Should say:** make both records say the same thing — "Japanese from 1914; mandate conferred by the League in December 1920".

**Confidence:** high that the inconsistency exists.

### A26. Hong Kong's note skips the cession of Kowloon

**Location:** `data.js:266–270` (1930): "Ceded in 1842 and enlarged by the New Territories lease of 1898."

**What is wrong:** the colony was built in three steps — Hong Kong Island ceded 1842, **Kowloon south of Boundary Street ceded by the Convention of Peking in 1860**, the New Territories leased for 99 years in 1898. Skipping 1860 makes the New Territories look like the only enlargement, and the map's gazetteer has a separate Kowloon marker.

**Should say:** "Hong Kong Island ceded in 1842, Kowloon in 1860, and the New Territories leased for ninety-nine years in 1898."

**Confidence:** high.

## B. MATTERS OF JUDGEMENT

### B1. Manchukuo's provinces are the scheme of 1934–37, shown on a December 1942 map

**Location:** `data.js:1871–1901`; `SOURCES.md:236`; `sources.html:227–234`.

Fourteen provinces (ten plus the four Hsingan) is what the 1935 SMR sheet shows, and using that sheet is a defensible, well-documented decision. But by December 1942 Manchukuo had **nineteen** provinces plus Hsinking Special City: Tunghua and Mutankiang were created in 1937, Tungan and Peian in 1939, Ssupingkai in 1941. A map that elsewhere refuses to let 1942 prose stand on the 1930 map should say, in one clause, that these are the provinces of 1934–37 and that five more had been carved out by the map's date. The `SOURCES.md` confidence rating ("Very good") does not mention the seven-year gap.

**Suggested wording:** in the `JMAP.PROVINCES` comment and in `SOURCES.md`: "the provinces as the 1935 sheet draws them; Manchukuo went on subdividing, and by 1942 there were nineteen."

**Confidence:** high on the fact; the decision itself is yours.

---

### B2. Wake: "the only time in the war an amphibious assault was thrown back at the water's edge"

**Location:** `data.js:661–665`.

The claim as usually made is narrower — "the only amphibious assault repulsed by shore-based guns during the Second World War", or "the first amphibious assault of the Pacific War to be defeated". As written it invites a student to raise Milne Bay (August–September 1942) or the Japanese landings turned back off Wake by other means, and to conclude the map overstates. Adding four words ("thrown back at the water's edge by shore batteries") keeps the punch and removes the target.

---

### B3. Yenan as "the end of the Long March"

**Location:** `data.js:1104–1108`, `date: 'Communist base 1936–1947'`, note "The end of the Long March and the centre of Communist resistance".

The First Front Army's Long March ended in **October 1935** at Wuqi in northern Shensi; the party's centre was at Pao-an through 1936 and moved into Yenan only in December 1936 / January 1937. Yenan is the destination in the popular telling, not in the chronology. Consider "the base the survivors of the Long March settled into, and the centre of Communist resistance".

---

### B4. Nomonhan: "a Japanese army" against "a Japanese division"

**Location:** `data.js:828` (USSR, 1942): "where Zhukov destroyed a Japanese army"; against `data.js:1037–1041` (`id: 'nomonhan'`): "Zhukov's encirclement destroyed a Japanese division".

The second is right (the 23rd Division). "A Japanese army" in the other record is either loose English or a claim about the Kwantung Army as a whole. Make them agree.

---

### B5. Wade-Giles for Chinese people, except in two records

**Location:** `data.js:203–208` ("**Jin Shuren** was recognised by Nanking") and `data.js:834–839` ("ruled largely autonomously by **Sheng Shicai** from 1933").

Elsewhere the prose is consistently Wade-Giles or postal — Chang Hsüeh-liang, Chang Tso-lin, Chiang Kai-shek, Wang Ching-wei, Fu Tso-yi, P'u-i, Ba Maw. For consistency these would be **Chin Shu-jen** and **Sheng Shih-ts'ai** (or, if you prefer pinyin for people throughout, the change has to go the other way in six or seven records). Either is defensible; the mixture is not.

---

### B6. 広東 (Kanton) for the city of Canton

**Location:** `data.js:1114–1118`, `id: 'guangzhou'`, `ja: '広東 (Kanton)'`.

広東 is the *province* (Kwangtung); the city is 広州 (Kōshū). Japanese usage of the period did often call the city 広東/カントン, so this is not simply wrong, but on a map that also labels the province it will read as a mistake. Consider `'広州 (Kōshū)'`, or `'広東 (Kanton) — 広州'`.

---

### B7. 新南群島 on the 1930 map

**Location:** `data.js:395–400`, `id: 'spratly'` (1930 epoch), `ja: '新南群島 (Shinnan Guntō)'`.

Japan coined 新南群島 when it annexed the group to Takao prefecture in March 1939. On the 1930 map it is an anachronism of the kind the map is otherwise scrupulous about. Either drop the `ja` field for 1930 or gloss it ("named 新南群島 by Japan in 1939").

---

### B8. Tarawa's "two square kilometres"

**Location:** `data.js:1256–1260`. Betio is about **1.2 km²** (291 acres). "Barely more than a square kilometre" is both truer and more striking.

---

### B9. "Jìnán — southern Hopei"

**Location:** `data.js:1910`, `'Jinan': { en: 'Jìnán — southern Hopei', zh: '冀南' }`.

冀南 (Chi-nan) and 濟南 (Tsinan) are homographs in toneless pinyin and the map carries both, the second as a major city 250 km away. A reader who meets "Jìnán — southern Hopei" will think of the Shantung capital. Consider "Chi-nan (冀南) — southern Hopei" or "the Southern Hopei base area".

### B10. Labuan is grouped with North Borneo in 1930

**Location:** `data.js:256–260`, `id: 'northborneo'`, `en: 'North Borneo (and Labuan)'`.

The note is right that Labuan went to the Straits Settlements in 1907 — and `data.js:2172` says so twice over ("a Straits Settlement from 1907 until 1946, **not company territory**"). Titling the 1930 record "North Borneo (and Labuan)" nonetheless puts it with the chartered company. Since the 1942 record correctly groups it under the Japanese Borneo administration, the 1930 title is the odd one out.

### B11. Smaller wording points

- **`data.js:225`** — "The Andaman and Nicobar Islands were run from Calcutta as a penal settlement." True of the nineteenth century; by 1930 the Chief Commissioner answered to the Government of India at Delhi/Simla. "Run from India as a penal settlement" avoids the trap.
- **`data.js:1333–1335`** (harbin, 1930) — "a large émigré population, the biggest in the Russian Far East". Harbin is not in the Russian Far East; the sense is "the largest Russian community outside Soviet territory", which is the more striking claim anyway.
- **`data.js:560`** — `when: 'Japanese occupied. Nominally independent from March, 1932.'` The comma in "March, 1932" and the two full stops are out of house style for the `when` lines, which are elsewhere a single phrase.
- **`data.js:1049–1053`** (beijing) — `date: '… occupied July 1937'`. Chinese forces withdrew on 28–30 July; the Japanese army entered Peiping on 8 August. "Occupied July–August 1937" is safer.
- **`data.js:1222–1226`** (midway) — "Four Japanese fleet carriers sunk in a single day": three were fatally hit on 4 June and *Hiryū* on the 5th. "Four Japanese fleet carriers lost in two days" or "all four fatally hit within a day".
- **`data.js:154`/`529`** — `zh: '樺太（南薩哈林）'`. 南薩哈林 is a transliteration of the Russian; Chinese would normally say 庫頁島南部 (or 南庫頁島). Worth a second opinion if the Chinese column matters here.
- **`cities-gaz.js` 1942** — `yanan … "of":"Shen-Kan-Ning Border Region"` against `data.js:1907` `'Shaan-Gan-Ning'` / `Shǎngānníng`. Same region, two romanisation systems, two files.
- **`data.js:792`** (`id: 'ccp'`) — the note uses Wade-Giles ("Chin-Ch'a-Chi", "Chin-Chi-Lu-Yü") while the labels the reader will hover use pinyin with tones ("Jìnchájì", "Jìlǔyù"). Deliberate or not, the two are read side by side.
- **`SOURCES.md:240`** — "the 1898 lease boundary was a surveyed line across the isthmus" for the Kwantung leasehold. The Pulandian–Pikou line is the boundary as it stood under the Japanese leasehold after 1905; the Russian arrangement of 1898 included a neutral zone reaching much further north. For a map dated 1930 and 1942 the line is right and the attribution to 1898 is not.
- **`SOURCES.md:95`, `SOURCES.md:103`** — "Jagang", "Jeju" are Revised Romanisation in a file that promises McCune-Reischauer (Chagang, Cheju).
