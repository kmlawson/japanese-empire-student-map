Scope: `texts/territories/sub-units/{korea,korea-islands,taiwan,manchukuo,mengjiang}.csv/.md`
(edited directly); nine 1930 and seven 1942 territory records and 125 city/site records

## Mechanism check against map.js

The brief's description matches the code. `splitGloss` (map.js:680) splits on ` — ` only;
`shortOf` (map.js:984) returns `r.short`, or a gloss of ≤ 88 characters; `hoverShort`
(map.js:967) strips a trailing `pop` string and passes the rest to `briefOf` (map.js:945),
which returns the text unchanged at ≤ 120 characters and otherwise cuts at the first
sentence end / `;` / `:` / ` — ` past character 30. The card builder (map.js:10533) takes
`head.note || split.gloss || shortOf(head)` for a sub-unit — so **a `.md` note suppresses
`short` on the card entirely**. That governs every move below: text may leave `short` only
if it lands in the note, and a note may not be added to a record whose `short` carries facts
the note does not.

`briefOf` is where the over-long `short` cells do visible damage. Where a short opens with a
stub sentence — `The whole prefecture.` or `In Takao-shū (高雄州).` — the sentence end falls
before character 30, the `:` rule fires next, and the hover is left saying
`The whole prefecture. The far south` or `In Takao-shū (高雄州). Drawn as a fragment`. I ran
`briefOf` over every `short` in scope rather than guessing which were broken; that measurement
picked out exactly the 13 Taiwan rows the coordinator counted.

One correction to the brief's framing: **none of the Taiwan `short` cells is a paragraph.**
They are single clauses carrying the author's deliberate `In <prefecture> (漢字). ` prefix,
which the file's own commentary explains. I left that prefix alone everywhere and cut only to
get under the 120-character `briefOf` threshold. Manchukuo and Mengjiang are the opposite
case: every one of their 17 `short` cells is a full card paragraph sitting in a tooltip field.

## Link sweep

All 166 distinct `wiki` URLs in scope were resolved against the MediaWiki API with
`User-Agent: interactive-japan-map/1.0 (link check)`: **no dead links, no disambiguation

link whose title looked like it might name the wrong thing was then read; those are listed

### Flagged, not changed

* **Four Taiwanese city dots link to their prefecture, not their city** — `city-names`
  `taichung` → `Taichū_Prefecture`, `hsinchu` → `Shinchiku_Prefecture`, `hualien` →
  `Karenkō_Prefecture`, and `sites` `taipei` → `Taihoku_Prefecture` — while `keelung`,
  `tainan`, `chiayi`, `changhua`, `pingtung`, `taitung`, `makung` and `sites` `kaohsiung` all
  link to the city. None of the four is a wrong subject (each prefecture is named for and
  contains its city, and **`Taihoku` itself redirects to `Taihoku Prefecture`**, so that is the
  article for the colonial name the record uses), and `taichung`'s note is explicitly
  prefecture-framed — "the prefecture in which the Musha rising broke out". Which convention
  the gazetteer should follow is the author's call, not a link error, so I left all four and
  raise it here.
* **`city-names` `wushe` → `Musha_Incident`.** A settlement record pointing at an event
  article. There is no English article for the village; the township article, `Ren'ai, Nantou`,
  is a different administrative unit of 15,670 people. The note is itself mostly about the
  rising, so the link is not misleading, and `sites` `musha` uses the same article correctly.
* **`sites` `wonsanstrike` and `erlin` have no `wiki`, correctly.** `Wonsan general strike`
  does not exist in English and `Erlin` is a disambiguation page; blank is right for both.
* **Two readings of 野口遵.** `sites`/`city-names` `hungnam` says "Noguchi Jun's chemical
  combine"; `sub-units/korea.md` `Kankyonan` says "Noguchi Shitagau's Chōsen Chisso". Both
  readings are attested and neither is wrong, but the map says it two ways.
* **Two summarising last sentences.** `sites` `huanggutun` ends "which is most of why 1931
  happened the way it did" and `portarthursiege` ends "The scale of the losses set the pattern
  the next war would follow." Both are evaluations rather than facts, which question 3 would
