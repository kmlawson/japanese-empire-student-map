### Two claims corrected

* **`Karenni States`, the `short`.** It read "Four states in treaty relations
  with the Crown, never annexed and never part of Burma proper." — which
  contradicts the note beside it ("Three small states — Kantarawadi, Bawlake and
  Kyebogyi"). `Karenni_States` settles it: Britain recognised **four** states in
  1875 (Kyebogyi, Nammekon, Naungpale, Bawlake) with **Kantarawadi separate**,
  and by the period this map draws there were **three** administrations —
  "On 10 October 1922 the administrations of the Karenni states (Bawlake,
  Kantarawaddy, and Kyebogyi) were placed under the administration of the
  Federated Shan States". The note is right; the number in the `short` was not.
  Rewritten as a phrase without a number: `Never annexed, and never part of
  Burma proper` (it also carried a full stop, which a `short` should not).
* **`Kantarawadi`'s new note** says Britain granted it no protection in 1875,
  from the same article: "the British granted recognition to four states …
  Kantarawadi state, however, remained independent without official protection".

### One claim checked and kept

`burma.md`'s Kengtung note says its ruler "was one of only four Shan chiefs
granted a permanent nine-gun salute in 1903, with Hsipaw, Yawnghwe and Möng
Nai". Two of the four are confirmed — `Hsipaw_State`: "the ruler of Hsipaw State
was entitled to a nine-gun salute"; `Yawnghwe`: "They were entitled to a 9-gun
salute by the British authorities" — and neither `Kengtung_State` nor `Möng_Nai`
mentions salutes either way. Nothing contradicts it, so it stays. The new notes
I wrote repeat the salute only for Hsipaw and Yawnghwe, where it is sourced, and
Möng Nai's note and short do not claim it.
