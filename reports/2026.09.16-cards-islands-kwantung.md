# Cards, second pass: `islands-kwantung`

Two tasks. Task 1 fills the island rows that lack a `short` or a note, in
sub-unit files edited directly. Task 2 fact-checks the Kwantung Leased
Territory, whose records are all in shared files and go through
`reports/2026.09.16-cards-islands-kwantung.changes.json`.

## Mechanism check against map.js

`shortOf` (map.js:984) returns `r.short`, and falls back to the ` — ` gloss in
`en` only when it is ≤ 88 characters (`SHORT_MAX`). `hoverShort` (967) passes
that through `briefOf` (945), which cuts at the first sentence past 120
characters. The sub-unit card text is `head.note || split.gloss || shortOf(head)`
(10533), so **once a row has a note, the gloss in `en` is dead text**: it can
only ever reach the hover, and only while `short` is empty. That is what the
eleven rows below were relying on. The brief's description matches the code.

### `texts/territories/1930.md` / `kwantung` — no change

> The tip of the Liaodong peninsula, leased by Russia in 1898 and transferred to
> Japan in 1905. Lüshun and Dalian sat inside it, and so did the garrison that
> became the Kwantung Army.

Every claim holds. <https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/関東州>: the 1898
旅順・大連租借に関する露清条約 leased it to Russia for 25 years; the Treaty of
Portsmouth (5 September 1905) transferred the Russian rights and the 満洲善後条約 of
22 December 1905 formalised the transfer; area 3,462.5 km².
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Leased_Territory> agrees ("March 1898 …
25 years", "Convention for the Lease of the Liaotung Peninsula"; the term
extended to 99 years in 1915 under the Twenty-One Demands) and names Dairen (the
capital), Ryojun/Port Arthur, Kinshū and Furanten as its cities.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Army>: the Kwantung Garrison was raised
in 1906 to guard the Leased Territory and the South Manchuria Railway Zone,
headquartered at Port Arthur, and in 1919 the Government-general was split into
a civilian bureau and the Kwantung Army — so "the garrison that became the
Kwantung Army" is right, and in 1930 its headquarters was still in the territory.

### `texts/territories/1942.md` / `kwantung` — **fact corrected**

Old:

> Nominally Manchukuo’s, in that the new state re-granted the lease in 1932; in
> practice a Japanese leasehold with its own administration to the end, **and the
> seat of the Kwantung Army** that had taken Manchuria. Lüshun and Dalian are
> inside it.

**The Kwantung Army was not seated in the Leased Territory in 1942.**
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Army>: headquarters at Port Arthur
1906–1932, then "Xinjing, Manchukuo (1932–1945)" — "its headquarters was
transferred to the new Manchukuo capital of Xinjing". On the 1942 sheet the
sentence is wrong. New text, in the changes JSON:

> Nominally Manchukuo’s, in that the new state re-granted the lease in 1932; in
> practice a Japanese leasehold with its own administration to the end. The
> Kwantung Army was formed here in 1919 and moved its headquarters to Hsinking
> in 1932. Lüshun and Dalian are inside it.

"Hsinking" is the form `texts/sites/sites.csv` already uses for `changchun`
(`Shinkyō (Chángchūn, Hsinking)`).

The 1932 re-grant itself checks out: <https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/関東州> — after
Manchukuo was set up in 1932 Japan reframed the lease as granted by the new state
rather than by China, with an administrative reorganisation in December 1934
(関東局 / 関東州庁); <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Leased_Territory> — "Japan
renegotiated the lease with the new puppet state but retained separate control of
the Leased Territory". So does "its own administration to the end": the seat was
at Dairen throughout, and the territory was Soviet-occupied from August 1945.

### `texts/sites/sites.md` / `portarthur` — **style**

Old second sentence: "The pivot of Japanese continental policy for fifty years."
That is an evaluation, not a fact, and is the grand summarising last sentence
question 3 names. Replaced with what the record is:

> Taken from China in 1894, given up under the Triple Intervention of 1895,
> leased by Russia in 1898, and won back at enormous cost in the siege of
> 1904–05. Japan held it as Ryojun from 1905 to 1945, a city of the Kwantung

The first sentence was checked and kept.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCshunkou,_Dalian>: Japanese capture 20–21

France, Germany and Russia forced it back; "The Russian Empire in 1898 coerced a
lease from China of the Liaodong Peninsula"; the siege ended 2 January 1905;
"Japan administered the territory as Ryojun" to 1945; Soviet occupation from
1945. "At enormous cost" is carried by the 57,780 Japanese casualties below.
`wiki` → `Lüshunkou,_Dalian` is the right place article and is kept.

### `texts/sites/sites.md` / `dairen` — **fact corrected and style**

Old: "The commercial capital of the Kwantung Leased Territory and headquarters of
the South Manchuria Railway Company, **the vehicle of Japanese economic power in
the region**."

Two things. The trailing appositive is evaluation and goes (question 3). And
"commercial capital" undersells it: Dairen was the capital outright.
<https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/関東州> — the 関東都督府 (1906) → 関東庁 (1919) → 関東局/関東州庁
(1934) were all "headquartered in Dalian";
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Leased_Territory> lists "Dairen

> The capital of the Kwantung Leased Territory, and the headquarters of the South
> Manchuria Railway Company from 1907.

The railway company's seat is confirmed by
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Manchuria_Railway>: founded 26 November
1906, "the headquarters was established in Tokyo before relocated to Dalian in
1907". `wiki` → `Dalian` is the article for the place and is kept.

### `texts/sites/sites.md` / `portarthursiege` — **style**

Old last sentence: "The scale of the losses set the pattern the next war would
follow." Unverifiable and a summarising flourish; removed. What is left was
checked and is exact:

> Five months, eleven-inch howitzers firing on the harbour, and something like
> sixty thousand Japanese casualties for a fortress that surrendered on 2 January

<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Port_Arthur>: "August 1, 1904 – January
2, 1905", "5 months and 1 day"; "57,780 casualties (killed, wounded and
missing)"; "11-inch (280 mm) siege howitzers" firing on "the Russian fleet
sheltering" in the harbour after 203 Metre Hill fell. The author's hedge
"something like sixty thousand" is right and is kept rather than swapped for the
figure. The `date` cell, "August 1904 – 2 January 1905", is correct; `wiki` →
`Siege_of_Port_Arthur` is correct. Both kept.

### Two source discrepancies, recorded but not acted on

Neither touches any note text.

* **The railway zone.** `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Leased_Territory` says
  the extraterritorial rights along the South Manchuria Railway "transferred to
  Manchukuo in 1932". Both
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Manchuria_Railway_Zone> ("abolished in
  1937") and <https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/関東州> (附属地 administrative authority
  to Manchukuo in 1937) say 1937, which is right. No note on the map says
  otherwise — the zone was separate from the Leased Territory, was never part of
  it, and none of these records mentions it — so there is nothing to correct.
* **Population.** The English article gives the 1935 census as 1,034,074; the
  Japanese article gives 1,134,074 (168,185 Japanese and Korean, 963,875
  Chinese/Manchurian — which sums to 1,132,060, so neither total reconciles
  cleanly). No figure appears in any of these records and none was added.
