# The station names: what is missing, and what is not

*An audit, 4 September 2026. Nothing in the data was changed by it — see
"What I have not done" at the foot.*

## The headline: no station anywhere is missing its characters

Every one of the 2,545 station records in the four tables carries its 漢字 /
한자. So the part of the ask that was "when missing kanji look for it in the
station name website" has nothing to do.

| table | stations | no characters | no romaji | no pinyin / M–R | no kana / hangul |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kr-stations.js` — the map layer | 850 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `tw-stations.js` — the map layer | 206 | **0** | 52 | 1 | 119 |
| `kr-trains.js` — the timetable | 1,302 | **0** | 605 | 605 | 605 |
| `tw-trains.js` — the timetable | 187 | **0** | 63 | 1 | 108 |

Korea's map layer is complete on all four fields, which is worth saying plainly:
850 stations in hangul, hanja, McCune–Reischauer and the Japanese reading, none
of them guessed.

## Where the gaps actually are

**Taiwan has no Japanese reading for 72 distinct stations.** That is the union
of the two Taiwan tables — 52 in the map layer, 63 in the timetable bundle, 43
of them in both. Every one has its characters and its pinyin, so what is absent
is only the reading.

The build says so on purpose. `tools/build_tw_stations.py` writes at the head of
its output: *"`ro` is empty where none does, and the map shows the hanji rather
than a guess."* Filling these is a change of that policy, not an omission being
repaired, which is why they are listed here rather than written.

**Korea's timetable bundle is short on 605 rows, and 520 of those draw nothing.**

network the Korean tables connect to; those stations are not Korean and have no
hangul, no M–R and no Japanese reading in the source. 782 of the 1,302 are
placed on the map, and of those **85 are short** — the through-connection stops
at Mukden, Hsinking, Harbin, Peking, Shimonoseki and the rest.

## The mechanical fix that turned out not to exist

Both Taiwan tables show one station with no pinyin, and it should not have one:
**宮ノ下 Miyanoshita** (`tws206`), a station named in Japanese, katakana ノ and
all. There is no Chinese reading of it to convert. The blank is the right
answer and it stays.

Korea's McCune–Reischauer is complete. So the part of the ask that expected
"pinyin / M–R should be easy to convert from hanzi" has nothing in it: there is
no station in either colony missing the romanisation of its own language.

## What I have not done, and why

**I have not written 72 tentative Japanese readings into the data.** Most are
ordinary on-yomi compounds a reader could predict — 日南 Nichinan, 清水
Kiyomizu, 豐原 Toyohara, 桃園 Tōen — but Taiwanese station names under Japanese
rule are exactly the class where the predictable answer is often wrong: 打狗
became 高雄 *Takao*, 阿緱 became 屏東 *Heitō*, and a reading worked out from the
characters would have missed both. Several on this list are of that kind — 唭里岸,
番子田, 鼻子頭, 車路墘 — and three are Japanese-era ward names (大正街, 老松町,
東門町) whose readings are certain but whose *stations* want checking against the
timetable rather than against a modern gazetteer.

Written unconfirmed at the end of a long session, seventy-two of those become
seventy-two things that look sourced. The list above is what a session with the
station-name site open needs; the field to mark them with, if they are filled
tentatively, should be a flag of its own so the map can say "reading not
confirmed" rather than presenting a guess as a reading.
