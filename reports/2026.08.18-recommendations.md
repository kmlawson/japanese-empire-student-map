# Pan and zoom: where the time goes, and what could be done about it

**Written 18 August 2026 from three independent reviews. Rewritten 20 August after four
of its recommendations shipped, from two further reviews — one profiling with exclusive
use of the machine, one reading the files without benchmarking so as not to corrupt it.**

The 18 August document is superseded rather than appended to, because most of its
headline numbers are no longer true: the map now runs at roughly one-eighth to one-third
of the cost it measured, and the ranking of what is expensive has completely reshuffled.
Where a conclusion has been overturned, this document says so and says by what.

---

## The short version, 20 August

Four things shipped, and between them they took the everyday views from 70–192
raster-milliseconds a frame to 11–29:

| desktop pan, raster ms/frame | 18 Aug | today | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1930 China + admin | 70.0 | **10.9** | 16% |
| 1942 China + admin + extent | 99.4 | **29.3** | 29% |
| whole empire 1942 | 192 | **23.2** | 12% |
| India 1930 + admin | 148 | **16.5** | 11% |

Those were: mitred joins, the 1.3 px land stroke off by default behind a Layers switch,
`dropForGesture()` dropping the selection on drag/pinch/wheel, and — incidentally — the
NCA source suppressing the extent line.

**What is expensive now is one element.** `#extent-1942`, the dashed 1942 line of
control, is **38% of a desktop fit-view pan frame, 55% of a zoom frame, 20% of a mobile
frame, and 99% of a deep-zoom frame**. Culling its off-screen subpaths costs 0.02–0.10 ms
of JavaScript per frame and takes the one view in this map that measurably stutters —
desktop deep zoom, 245 ms/frame, 16 fps, half its frames missing two budgets — to 60 fps.

**The largest lever is unchanged**: pan by a composited CSS transform instead of
rewriting the `viewBox`. Re-measured at **15–18× on raster and 20 tiles/frame → 1.4**, on
both profiles, with `will-change` still load-bearing.

**And for the question behind all of this — room for new layers** — the budget is
about **290,000 more filled vertices on desktop and 500,000 on the phone**, or a seventh
of that if the new layer is stroked. After the composited transform it is at least a
million and probably several.

## 1. The current baseline

dsf 2; mobile 390×844 dsf 3 with a 4× main-thread throttle. A fixed CPU canary sits

## 2. Where the time goes now

Interleaved control → variant → control, two rounds, order rotated. 1942 China + admin +
extent, desktop pan, control 30.45 ms/f (32 controls spanning 29.4–31.2, which is the
noise floor).

| variant | % of control | what it means |
|---|---:|---|
| `#land` hidden | 53% | all land = 14.3 ms |
| all strokes off | 60% | every stroke in the document = 12.2 ms |
| **extent line off** | **62%** | **one element = 11.6 ms, 38% of the frame** |
| `shape-rendering: optimizeSpeed` | 82% | antialiasing = 5.5 ms *(visible change)* |
| **clip-paths off** | **88%** | **3.7 ms — no longer free** |
| `#backings` hidden | 89% | 3.4 ms |
| `#seams` hidden | 98% | at noise |
| `#sub-outlines` off | 99% | at noise |
| `#atom-hits`, hatch flattened, `#ocean`, `#labels`, `#gaz`, `#markers` | 100–103% | at noise |
| **hairline switched ON** | **245%** | *adds* 1.45× the whole frame |

All the remaining stroke cost is the extent line: strokes-off (60%) and extent-off (62%)
are the same number. Other views, same design:

* **whole empire, desktop pan** — `#land` 45%, strokes 64%, `#backings` 77%, extent 77%, `#seams` 92%, clips 95%, hairline ON **380%**.
* **1942 China, desktop zoom** — strokes 44%, **extent 45%**, `#land` 67%, clips 90%, `#backings` 95%, hairline ON 185%.
* **1942 China, mobile pan** — `#land` **30%**, shape-rendering 71%, strokes 77%, **extent 80%**, `#backings` 80%, clips 86%, hairline ON **369%**.
* **deep zoom, both profiles** — **extent off = 1–2%**, everything else 96–105%. At depth the line of control is 99% of the raster cost, and in that view it draws nothing at all: 0 of its 146 chunks intersect the screen.

### Why one dashed line costs this much

`#extent-1942` is 1,143 vertices in 6 subpaths, 8,897 user units long, `fill: none`,
`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, `stroke-width: 1.8`, `stroke-dasharray: 9 6`, round
caps and joins (`styles.css:451`). Cost scales with the **number of dash segments
generated over the whole path in device space**, which grows linearly with zoom: about
1,400 dashes at fit view and about **27,000 at 45×** — every frame, for a line almost
entirely off screen.

Deep zoom, desktop, control = line hidden (1.30–1.41 ms/f):

| | ms/f | × control |
|---|---:|---:|
| as built | 246.4 | 174–193× |
| same dash, *scaling* stroke (`vector-effect:none`, width and dash × k) | ~250 | 178× |
| butt caps, mitre joins | ~180 | 130× |
| `vector-effect:none`, dash left in user units | 9.7 | 6.9× |
| no dash at all | 6.5 | 4.9× |
| split into 8- or 32-point subpaths, **not** culled | 231 / 229 | 177 / 175× |
| **subpaths whose bbox misses the view dropped** | **1.3** | **0.99×** |

Two conclusions, both important. **It is not the `vector-effect`** — an arithmetically
identical scaling stroke costs the same. **And Skia does not cull dashing per contour** —
chopping the path into 146 pieces changes nothing; the pieces have to be *removed from
the DOM*.

## 3. Jank — the "zaggedness" question

Real mouse and touch drags, 60 moves, three runs per view. Dropped frames counted as rAF
intervals over two frame budgets (32 ms) and three (50 ms).

| desktop, mouse drag | rAF median | p95 | worst | >32 ms | >50 ms | raster/f |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1942 China + admin + ext | 16.7 | 16.8 | 50.0 | 2/115 | 0 | 17.6 |
| 1930 China + admin | 16.7 | 16.8 | 66.6 | 2/116 | 1 | 10.1 |
| whole empire 1942 | 16.7 | 16.8 | 16.8 | 0/112 | 0 | 9.4 |
| **deep zoom Okinawa** | 16.8 | **66.7** | 66.8 | **61/123** | **48** | 120.3 |

| mobile, touch drag | rAF median | p95 | worst | >32 ms | raster/f |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1942 China + admin + ext | 16.7 | 16.8 | 16.8 | 0/113 | 8.3 |
| 1930 China + admin | 16.7 | 16.7 | 16.8 | 0/112 | 4.4 |
| whole empire 1942 | 16.7 | 16.8 | 16.8 | 0/118 | 5.6 |
| deep zoom Okinawa | 16.7 | 16.8 | 33.4 | 2/138 | 14.6 |

In the four everyday views the cadence is metronomic — median 16.7, p95 16.8, at most two
isolated stalls per drag. **All the measurable stutter is in one view: desktop deep zoom**,
where half the frames miss two budgets, 48 of 123 miss three, and the scripted gesture
runs at 16 fps. Culling the extent line takes that view from 58.8 to 16.5 ms/frame — 17 fps
to 60 — and is the single change that removes measured jank from this map.

**Read the mobile zeros carefully.** Raster runs on worker threads, so a slow frame only
becomes a long rAF interval once raster saturates and back-pressures the main thread,
which on this box happens around 60–65 raster-ms/frame. The mobile profile throttles only
the main thread; a real handset throttles its raster workers too. **These counts are a
lower bound**, and "0 dropped frames" means "not detectable on this instrument", not
"smooth on a real phone". The pinch path was never exercised, and Puppeteer delivers
pointer moves more evenly than a finger, which suppresses input jitter.

## 4. Headroom: what a new layer costs

Paths intersecting the viewport are cloned into a new layer, jittered, and swept 1×–8×,
each level sandwiched between controls. 1942 China + admin + extent.

| | desktop | mobile |
|---|---:|---:|
| filled geometry | **0.103 ms per 1,000 vertices** | **0.078** |
| stroked geometry, 1.3 px non-scaling | **0.694 ms per 1,000** | **0.563** |
| element overhead | 0.44–1.5 ms per 1,000 elements | 0.65–0.88 |
| smoothness knee | wall/frame leaves 16.8 at ≈ **55–65 raster ms/frame** | same |

Linear in vertices over an 8× range on both profiles. Three things follow:

* **A stroked layer costs seven times a filled one.** 0.694 against 0.103 ms per thousand vertices. If a new layer can be drawn as fill alone, draw it as fill alone.
* **Elements are nearly free on desktop and not on the phone.** 6,000 extra elements carrying 24 k vertices cost 3.5 ms and no wall-clock on desktop; on the throttled phone the same 6,000 push wall/frame from 16.7 to **22.1** while raster rises only 4 ms. That is main-thread display-item recording. **Keep a new layer under roughly 2,000 elements** or the phone loses 60 fps long before the raster budget is spent.
* **Today's budget.** The everyday 1942 view spends 29–30 ms desktop and 16.4 mobile against a ~60 ms ceiling, so **≈ 290,000 more filled vertices on desktop and ≈ 500,000 on the phone — or 43,000 / 70,000 stroked.** For scale the document paints 293,447 vertices in that view and 141,630 of them meet the viewport: the map can carry roughly **one more copy of itself in fills, a seventh of itself in strokes.** Removing the extent line's 11.6 ms first takes the desktop figure from ~290 k to ~400 k.

**And the budget changes character entirely if the composited transform lands first:**

| | desktop | mobile |
|---|---:|---:|
| control | 1.52 raster/f, wall 16.4 | 0.91, wall 16.4 |
| +223 k / +247 k vertices | 2.92, wall 16.3 | 2.90, wall 16.3 |
| **+891 k / +990 k vertices** | **9.32, wall 16.7** | **7.13, wall 16.3** |

Adding 891,000 vertices costs 7.8 ms/frame instead of 91, the marginal cost falls from
0.103 to about **0.009 ms per 1,000 vertices — 12× cheaper** — and the cadence never
moves. The sweep stopped at 891 k, so no number beyond "at least a million, comfortably"
is claimed.

**The NCA layers, as a worked example.** 4,669 added vertices in 137 subpaths, two plain
fills. Predicted +0.48 ms from the slope; measured **+0.9 desktop, +0.8 mobile** — the
gap is area, since these are two large solid shapes and the slope was fitted on
coastlines. 3–5% of a frame, at the noise edge. And **hidden geometry is genuinely free**:
detaching nine hidden subtrees from the document moved the frame 0–1%, inside a ±3%
control spread.

## 5. What to do, ranked

### Tier A — measured, cheap, no cartographic decision

**A1. Cull `#extent-1942` to the viewport.** Pre-chunk the line into subpaths at build
time, drop the ones whose bounding box misses the view, restore them on view change.
Measured: **0.02–0.10 ms/frame of JavaScript**, and

| | as built | culled | hidden |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| desktop, 1942 China fit view | 29.8 | **22.3** | 18.2 |
| desktop, deep zoom | 242.6 (17 fps) | **7.4 (60 fps)** | 7.8 |
| mobile, 1942 China fit view | 16.5 | 17.2 | 13.3 |

25% of a desktop fit-view frame, 97% of a deep-zoom frame, nothing at the phone's fit
view (its narrow viewport already keeps most chunks). **Risk:** chunking restarts the dash
phase at each join, so the dash pattern shifts slightly at chunk boundaries — pixel-diff
before shipping. This is the single most valuable change on the list and it is not a
cartographic decision.

**A2. Stop `rescale()` writing to elements nobody can see.** Measured **1,643 transform
writes per wheel frame on desktop, 1,479 on mobile, of which 88% go to unpainted
elements** — worse than the 40% estimated in August. Plus **7.0 / 6.4
`getBoundingClientRect` calls per wheel frame costing 2.19 ms desktop and 7.49 ms mobile**
of forced layout. This is now specifically a *zoom* problem: on the pan path `rescale()`
does not run and the whole of the app's JS is ~0.5 ms/step. On the phone the wheel path is
the dominant cost of the whole map.

**A3. Delete the dead `#browse` layer.** 340 nodes and 170 `scalables` entries that are
`display:none` for the life of the page — `buildBrowse()` (`map.js:592`) builds them, and
both display tests read `(!JMAP.GAZ && browseVisible())` while `JMAP.GAZ` is always
defined. Part of A2's 88%.

**A4. Payload items, none of which touch a frame time** — see §7.

### Tier B — the large project

**B1. Pan by a composited CSS transform.** Re-measured 20 August:

| | desktop raster/f | tiles/f | mobile raster/f | tiles/f |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1942 China, viewBox | 29.7 | 20.8 | 16.4 | 8.00 |
| 1942 China, **transform + will-change** | **1.90** | **1.40** | **0.94** | **0.75** |
| 1942 China, transform *without* will-change | 32.0 | 20.4 | 20.0 | 8.35 |
| whole empire, viewBox → will-change | 23.7 → **1.46** | | 11.6 → **0.62** | |
| 1930 China, viewBox → will-change | 11.1 → **0.66** | | 8.0 → **0.45** | |

15–18× on raster, 20 tiles/frame → 1.4, on both profiles. `will-change` is load-bearing:
without it the transform is *worse* than the viewBox. Needs the SVG box over-rendered by a
margin, `overflow: hidden` on the container, and a commit to `viewBox` on `pointerup`.
Pixel-identical for a translate; a composited **zoom** is a resampled upscale and goes soft
during the gesture, so pan and zoom should be decided separately. None of the correctness
work was tested.

### Tier C — now worth re-costing as frame-rate items

**C1. The clip-paths are no longer free.** Removing every `clip-path`: desktop 1942 pan
**88%**, desktop zoom 90%, mobile pan **86%**, desktop fit view with the extent line
already hidden **78%**, deep zoom 100%. In August this measured 93% and was called
"indistinguishable from zero" — true then, because strokes were 85% of the frame and hid
it. They are now **12–22% of a fit-view frame**. `clip-china` is 16,406 vertices;
`clip-off-clients` is 5,516 and is 100% duplicate geometry (§7). **This revives the
build-time polygon boolean as a performance argument, not only a payload one** — see the
disagreement noted in §8.

**C2. The hairline switch, when a reader turns it on**, costs 2.45× (desktop China 1942),
3.80× (whole empire), 3.69× (mobile). Nothing to do unless the default changes; recorded
so the cost of that decision is on the table.

## 6. Zaggedness — the visual half

`MAX_ZOOM` is 100 (`map.js:1252`), so the deepest view is 1.4° wide. What the reader sees
there is set by two quantities: the simplification tolerance and the grid the path is
*written* on.

| viewport | 0.1-unit grid @40× | @100× | 0.01 grid @40× | @100× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1600 CSS px, dsf 1 | 2.29 px | 5.71 px | 0.23 | 0.57 |
| 1600 CSS px, dsf 2 | **4.57 px** | **11.43 px** | 0.46 | 1.14 |
| 390 CSS px, dsf 3 | 1.67 px | **4.18 px** | 0.17 | 0.42 |

The project's stated standard — "0.021 units = half a pixel at the deepest zoom" — is half
a device pixel at 40× on a **1600 px, dsf 1** screen. On a retina desktop it is 0.96 px at
40× and 2.4 px at 100×. The standard is real but it was framed for one screen and one zoom.

**Correction to a premise both this document and `tasks.md` have repeated.**
`FINE_PRECISION` is *not* "for small shapes": it is passed for `FULL_DETAIL` and
`TRACED_TOL` atoms, and `FULL_DETAIL` includes `china`, `korea`, `manchukuo`, `princely`,
`tibet`, `xinjiang` and the GIS layers, while `TRACED_TOL` adds `india` and both NCA
layers. Measured, **47.5% of the main file's coordinates are already at two decimal
places** (admin 59.6%, roc 100%). China's fill is at 2 dp. India is at 2 dp — 90.1% of its
coordinates. Only Indochina, of the three mainlands named in August, is on the coarse grid.

**The real find is elsewhere.** The fine-coastline layer simplifies each ring at
`FINE_TOL_DEG = 0.002°` = 0.04 units and then writes it with a bare `ring_to_path(pts)`
(`build_map.py:6964`) — **no precision argument** — so any island wider than 2 units lands
on the 0.1-unit grid, **2.5× coarser than the tolerance that produced it**. Measured: 104
rings holding **13,549 vertices, 51% of the fine layer**, including Okinawa, Guadalcanal,
Bougainville, New Britain, New Ireland, Malaita, Choiseul, Santa Isabel, Amamioshima,
Singapore and Guam. At 100× on a retina desktop Okinawa's coast is drawn on an
11.4-device-pixel grid while its own simplification is accurate to 4.6. **The layer that
exists for deep zoom is grid-limited on every island a reader would zoom to.**

### The tolerance bands, in device pixels

| band | span | tolerance | @40× (1600/dsf2) | @100× | @100× phone |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | > 3° | 0.550 u | 25.1 | **62.9** | 23.0 |
| 2 | 12–60 u | 0.275 u | 12.6 | 31.4 | 11.5 |
| 3 | 2–12 u | 0.066 u | 3.0 | 7.5 | 2.8 |
| 4 | < 2 u | 0.0165 u | 0.75 | 1.9 | 0.7 |
| traced | — | 0.021 u | 0.96 | 2.4 | 0.9 |
| fine layer | — | 0.04 u | 1.8 | 4.6 | 1.7 |
| **write grid** | ≥ 2 u | **0.1 u** | **4.6** | **11.4** | **4.2** |

For band 1 the simplification is 5.5× the grid; for band 3 the grid is 1.5× the
simplification. **So the write grid is the dominant error precisely for the middle-sized
shapes** — the Visayas, the Moluccas, the Kuriles, the smaller Aleutians, the coastal
detail of Indochina and the Indies, 16,800+ vertices of it.

Two other things a reader can see and nobody has costed: **`#extent-1942` is simplified at
0.35 units — 40 device pixels at 100×, the coarsest drawn thing on the map** and a headline
feature; and the rivers at 0.2 units + Chaikin.

### Recommendation

| change | file:line | cost | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| pass `FINE_PRECISION` in the fine-layer writer | `build_map.py:6964` | +29 KB, **0 vertices** | fixes the deep-zoom layer's own islands |
| move `path_precision`'s cut-off from `span < 2` to `span < 12` | `build_map.py:3122` | ~+60 KB *(est)*, 0 vertices | fixes the band-3 population where the grid is the larger error |
| give `line_to_path` a precision argument | `build_map.py:3162` | ~+20 KB *(est)* | ends the mixed-grid coastline family, and lets the extent line be written finer |
| revisit `--tolerance 0.55` for the mainlands | `:4155`, `:5495` | large, and it is the raster bill | the only thing that touches the 63-device-pixel error |
| **do not** lower `MAX_ZOOM` | — | — | keeping the 0.1 grid at half a device pixel would need `MAX_ZOOM = 4.4`. Deep zoom is *by design* a view of approximate geometry (`map.js:1246`); extend the fine windows instead |
| **do not** apply blanket DP at 0.021 | — | — | 35,047 vertices saved is not worth reopening the flecks between every backing and its sub-units |

**Why raising precision is safe, measured rather than assumed.** The worry was
`ring_to_path`'s dedupe `eps`. Test: take five un-simplified `FULL_DETAIL` sources, apply
`simplify()` at each band tolerance, dedupe at 0.05 and at 0.005, compare. **Douglas–Peucker
at any band tolerance already leaves no adjacent pair within 0.05 units** — one vertex out
of 2,166 across the whole experiment. So for every DP-simplified atom, finer precision is
**pure bytes, zero vertices, zero raster.** It is *not* free for geometry that never goes
through DP — the seams, the occupied zone, and everything through `line_to_path`.

**And the flecks comment cuts *for* this, not against.** `build_map.py:3127-3135` records
that a *large* eps made two shapes cut from one coastline part company and show as flecks;
the fix already in the tree is a *smaller* one. The hazard is doing it to some members of a
coastline family and not others — and that family is **currently mixed**: `chinabase` 10.1%
at 2 dp, `#a-occupiedzone` 48.1%, `china` backing 78.1%, `clip-china` 84.2%, `.whole-edge`
and `.coast` 0%. Six shapes cut from one coastline on two grids. Whether that flecks at
100× is **untested** — it may be masked only because those last two draw nothing by default.

## 8. Where the two reviews disagree

**The clip-paths, and therefore the polygon boolean.** The file review demoted the
build-time boolean partly on August's finding that clips are free, and concluded "none of
this should change a frame time." The profiling review, measuring the same day, found
clips now cost **12–22% of a fit-view frame** on both profiles, because the strokes that
used to mask them are gone.

**The measurement wins.** `clip-china` should be re-costed as a frame-rate item worth
roughly 3.7 ms of a 30 ms desktop frame, not only as 233 KB of payload. Two caveats keep it
below Tier A: `clip-off-clients` (P4) is 26% of the clip geometry and is answered by a
two-line `<use>` rather than a boolean-geometry project; and the boolean's other old
justification — "it would end the split stroke" — is spent, because the split stroke is
`.whole-edge` + `.coast` and both are invisible by default.

## 9. Corrections to the 18 August document

1. **"The stroke is 73–86% of raster."** There is no land stroke by default. What remains of "strokes" is one element, `#extent-1942`.
2. **"Deep zoom is no longer expensive — 2.5 ms a frame."** Desktop deep zoom is **245 ms/frame and 16 fps**. That addendum measured a Ryukyus crop that happened to exclude the line of control.
3. **"`non-scaling-stroke` is 13× at deep zoom."** No longer true, and the mechanism was misattributed: an arithmetically identical scaling stroke costs the same. The driver is **dash-segment count in device space**.
4. **"The big clip-paths are free — leave them alone."** True in August, false now: 12–22% of a fit-view frame.
5. **"`applyView` recomputes the home view at ~4.5 ms/frame."** Not on the pan path (~0.5 ms/step). Still real on the **wheel** path, and there it is the phone's dominant cost.
6. **"~40% of transform writes go to invisible elements."** Measured **88%**.
7. **"Blink's own hit-test costs 0.30 ms per `pointermove`."** Not reproducible — `pointer-events: none` during a drag changes nothing measurable in four views on two profiles.
8. **`BACKING_FROM_SUBUNITS` has four members, not sixteen** (`build_map.py:736`). The August table listing sixteen atoms was measuring *all* backings against their sub-units, not the shared-arc candidates, and overstates the change's reach ~4×. The ~290 KB figure happens to be exactly those four.
9. **The shared-arc project's performance case is spent.** It was half-justified by deleting the stroke; the stroke is deleted. Detaching `#backings` entirely is now worth ~7% of a pan (20.5 → 19.0 ms, 1942 China) against 37% when all 42 paths were stroked. It survives as a 290 KB payload item with a correctness dividend — still the only proposal that closes cracks without moving a drawn point.
10. **`FINE_PRECISION` is not "for small shapes"**, and mainland China and India are already at 2 dp. What is wrongly on the coarse grid is the fine layer's own big islands.
11. **The stroke-width cliff** (declared units vs device pixels) is **still unresolved** and now much less urgent, the stroke being off by default.
12. **Every `map.js` line reference in the August document has drifted.** `viewBox` write `:1257→:1288`; `defaultView()` `:1188→:1219`; `rescale()` `:1336→:1367`; `showTooltip` `:2179→:2244`; `MAX_ZOOM` `:1221→:1252`; `buildBrowse` `:566→:592`.

## 10. Method, and what nobody has tested

Every ratio in §2, §4 and §5 is interleaved control → variant → control with the order
rotated, and the control-to-control spread quoted as the noise floor. Absolutes are quoted
only where the CPU canary was flat, and they reproduce across two sessions to within 3%.
The two reviews behind this rewrite were deliberately split so that only one of them
benchmarked: in August three agents measured at once and the canary drifted threefold.

Not tested, and worth knowing before acting:

* **Any real device, or GPU rasterisation.** All of this is headless-shell software raster on an M-series Mac. Ratios should port; absolutes should not. The mobile profile throttles only the main thread, so its jank figures are a lower bound.
* **Pixel identity of anything proposed here.** No screenshot diffs were run. In particular: the extent-line cull restarts the dash phase at chunk joins; merging the occupied zone's 732 same-label rings is arithmetically pixel-identical but has not been diffed; and the fine-precision fine layer's whole case rests on the change being *visible*, which nobody has looked at.
* **The pinch path** — touch drag only.
* **The composited pan's correctness work** — over-render margin, `overflow: hidden`, commit on `pointerup` — and whether a composited zoom is acceptable cartographically.
* **Whether the mixed-grid coastline family flecks at 100×** (§6).
* **Where the smoothness knee sits exactly** — bracketed between 53 and 76 raster-ms/frame on desktop and 36 and 55 on mobile, called 60, and a property of this machine's raster thread count.

**If one thing is done first**, it is A1: cull `#extent-1942` to the viewport. 0.02–0.10 ms
of JavaScript per frame, 25% of a desktop fit-view frame, and it converts the only view in
the map that measurably stutters from 17 fps to 60. B1, the composited transform, remains
the larger prize and the larger project.
