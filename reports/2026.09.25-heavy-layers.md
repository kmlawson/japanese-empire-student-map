# Heavy layers: what a pan and a zoom cost, and what was done about it

**Date:** 24–25 September 2026, against the working tree after update 373.
**Question asked:** would dimming and freezing the rest of the map while a
heavy thematic layer is up keep the map responsive? And then: make station
layers cheap at all times, and move the drawing as a picture during a gesture
whenever a thematic layer, the train tools or the plane tools are up.

## Method

Headless Chrome through `tools/test/suite.js`, 1280×900, CPU throttled to a
quarter (as `reports/2026.09.15-pan-zoom-performance.md`). Chrome trace
categories `toplevel`, `devtools.timeline` and
`disabled-by-default-devtools.timeline`; **busy** is the sum of `RunTask` on
the renderer main thread, **paint** the sum of `Paint` there. Every figure is
the median of three runs. The scripts were scratch files and are not kept;
this is what they did.

* **Gesture A:** a 40-step mouse drag, one animation frame per step, then
  twelve wheel steps (six in, six out), two frames each.
* **Gesture B:** 40 frames of pan with no map.js involvement: the viewBox
  written directly each frame, or a CSS transform written on `#map-svg`.
* **Synthetic layers**, drawn into the SVG where a theme goes: *400k points*
  (2,000 polygons of 200 jittered vertices, five opaque fills, over
  Manchuria); *8,000 shapes* (8,000 polygons of 8 vertices).
* **"Rest hidden"**: every top-level group of the SVG but the layer set to
  `display: none` — the most a freeze could save. **"Rest dimmed"**: a
  half-black rectangle over the map under the layer.

## 1. Dimming or freezing the rest does not pay

Gesture A, Manchuria view, busy ms:

| layer | as it was | rest hidden | rest dimmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (base map) | 968 | — | — |
| Manchuria railway only | 920 | — | — |
| Manchuria railway + stations | 1,343 | 1,182 | ≈1,343 |
| 400k points | 1,153 | 1,056 | 1,131 |
| 8,000 shapes | 1,633 | 1,641 | 1,614 |

A heavy layer's cost is its own. Taking everything else out of the drawing
saved 0–12%; the dim costs nothing and saves nothing. What drives the cost is
**how many separate elements** there are, not how many vertices: 400k
vertices in 2,000 shapes cost +185 ms, 64k vertices in 8,000 shapes +665 ms,
and Manchuria's 614 station marks +420 ms against the railway's own ≈0.

## 2. What does pay

Gesture B, busy ms:

| layer | viewBox per frame | one path per category | transform per frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,000 shapes | 636 | 250 | 65 |
| 400k points | 400 | 334 | 48 |
| Manchuria railway + stations | 405 | — | 119 |

## 3. What was done

* **Stations are a picture** (`drawStationPicture`, `liveStations`,
  `nearestStation` in map.js). Each railway's squares are two paths of
  square-capped strokes a hundredth of a map unit long, `non-scaling-stroke`,
  rebuilt when the set changes and never when the view does. Pressable
  `.sta-mark` elements exist only for stations in view (plus a quarter of the
  view each side), built at the settle, capped at 1,200; past the cap the
  pointer is answered from the table. Station counts for reference: Taiwan
  206, Karafuto 97, Manchuria 614, Korea 850, Japan 12,800 — 4,131 of them
  inside a view 2° tall.
* **The gesture is a picture** (`pictureMove` in map.js) while a thematic
  layer, the train tools or the plane tools are up: a CSS transform on
  `#map-svg`, the map drawn once 180 ms after the last change with no pointer
  down. Measured to agree with the redraw to 0.000 px at 1280×900 and
  390×664, by mouse, finger drag and pinch (`tools/test/picture.js`).

Gesture A against update 373 (served from a copy of `deploy/` with HEAD's
`lean/map.js` and `styles.css`), busy ms:

| state | update 373 | now | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan railway + stations, the Kantō | 8,330 | 2,292 | −72% |
| Manchuria train tools up | 1,395 | 560 | −60% |
| plane tools up | 959 | 387 | −60% |
| Burma theme | 1,648 | 734 | −55% |
| Manchuria railway + stations | 1,423 | 1,430 | 0 (under the cap: as before) |

## Not done, and why

* **Dimming the rest** — it saves nothing; worth doing only as a way of
  focusing the reader, not for speed.
* **One path per category for new thematic layers** — the Burma theme already
  is; a new layer should be built the same way (`tools/build_themes.py`).
* **Drawing past the edge** so a pan in picture mode does not show
  page-coloured ground until the redraw — possible (a larger SVG than the
  container), not attempted.
* **Station labels** are still one `<text>` per station, 12,800 for Japan,
  hidden until the view is under a degree tall; `gateLabels` walks them on
  every zoom frame. Not measured separately.

Unverified: Safari and Firefox. The square cap on a very short stroke and
`getScreenCTM` including a CSS transform on an ancestor are both specified
and both measured in Chrome only.
