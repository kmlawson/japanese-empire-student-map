Most of the coastlines and national outlines are originally Natural Earth, except China's, which is derived from the Republican province sheet below and nothing else; China's provinces are the real ones of 1928–45; Korea is the National Institute of Korean History's thirteen colonial provinces rather than modern units; the princely states of India were georeferenced by hand from a 1931 atlas; and the occupied zone and the line of control are traced from period maps and are approximations, as they say on the map itself.

  • Taiwan, 1930. The island's coastline and the divisions of the colony. Boundaries after 《日治時期臺灣行政區域沿革》, 1926年7月郡(市)界 (Academia Sinica RCHSS, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), reprojected by the author from TWD67 to TWD97 (EPSG:3826). Ajustments made: Areas labeled "蕃地" are drawn as one unit and named for the demarcation itself — the Taiwan Government-General's demarcated "Aborigine Territory" — rather than for the people the colonial state placed inside it. The distributed 1926 sheets of 《臺灣歷史文化地圖系統》 could not produce a complete administrative map: the 郡(市)界 layer omits 澎湖廳, collapses the two eastern 廳 and all 蕃地 into a single unnamed 19,000 km² residual, lacks 高雄市 and 基隆市, and carries schematic boundaries around 高雄; the corresponding 街(庄)界 layer is more degraded still. Claude Opus 4.5 was used to diagnose these problems and to reconstruct the layer used here from the 1920 街(庄)界 sheet, which is complete (304 units, including both eastern 廳, 澎湖郡, and 蕃地 resolved to 郡 level) and carries hierarchical 州/郡市/街庄 codes. There is an undeclared coordinate reference system fault affecting the whole series — the ESRI-style .prj names TWD67 without an EPSG authority code, so PROJ resolves it to an anonymous ellipsoidal CRS and applies a null datum shift, displacing the data approximately 830 m west. The layer was reprojected to TWD97 / TM2 zone 121 (EPSG:3826) via EPSG:3828, using the EPSG-registered transformation "TWD67 / TM2 zone 121 to TWD97 / TM2 zone 121 (1)". Two defective source records were repaired (新竹郡蕃地 lacked a name, 海山郡蕃地 an ID), and the documented administrative changes between July 1920 and January 1930 were applied: 基隆街 and 高雄街 raised to 市 and 高雄郡 dissolved, with 燕巢, 楠梓 and 左營庄 reassigned to 岡山郡 and 仁武庄 to 鳳山郡 (25 December 1924, 府令第90–91號); 澎湖郡 detached from 高雄州 as 澎湖廳 (July 1926); and 新竹街 and 嘉義街 raised to 市 (20 January 1930). Units were dissolved to 郡市 level with 蕃地 aggregated per 州廳, yielding 55 郡市 and 7 蕃地 blocks totalling 36,082 km², against 36,080 km² for the distributed 1926 sheet plus 澎湖. All processing was carried out in QGIS via PyQGIS scripts written by Claude.

  • The starting point of inspiration was the line of control drawn in Andrew Gordon's map in his textbook A Modern History of Japan, and modifications below were then begun from there to fit the December, 1942 date.

Held at Princeton University, call number G3201.A1.1927.H9 and may be viewed online here.
  • Japan's population, 1940 昭和十五年国勢調査人口 全国・道府県, printed pp. 17–19, from the 1940 statistical yearbook scanned and hosted by the Program for Constructing Data Infrastructure for the Humanities and Social Sciences, Institute of Economic Research, Hitotsubashi University (一橋大学経済研究所 人文学・社会科学データインフラストラクチャー構築推進事業). The forty-seven prefectures at the census of 1 October 1940, with the census's own division of every total into those registered in Japan proper (內地人), those registered in the 外地 (外地人) — 1,265,049 of them, chiefly Koreans — and foreign nationals, and beside them the service personnel, who are counted inside each total rather than added to it. Checked against the report's own arithmetic before it was used: the forty-seven sum to the printed 73,114,308 and to its male and female totals exactly, male plus female is the total in every row of all three tables, the three registers add to the total everywhere, and 銃後人口 plus 軍人軍属 does too. The report prints the country both with and without Okinawa, which was surveyed on a different footing; this map draws Okinawa, so it uses the 73,114,308 that includes it. The areas are the ones measured for 1930, both dates drawing the same forty-seven shapes. From the same yearbook, 第5表 民籍または国籍および男女別人口（全人口）pp. 362–363 gives the country as a whole by race or nationality, and Japan's card carries it: the four 外地 registers — 1,241,315 Koreans, 22,499 Taiwanese, 986 from Karafuto and 249 from the South Seas Mandate — and the seventy-five foreign nationalities, of which every one of a hundred or more is named, the source's own その他 kept as a line of its own, and the remaining fifty given in one line of 1,006. The 537 白系露人 are counted as a nationality of their own, stateless since 1921. Checked: the three registers add to 73,114,308, the four 外地 to 1,265,049, and the seventy-five nationalities to 39,237, in the total, the male and the female column alike.
  • Manchukuo's population, May 1943 滿洲國『臨時國勢調査報告』, 第一表 新京特別市及省別人口 p5, the density plate 第一圖 at the front of it, and 第二表 民族省別人口 pp6–7. 新京特別市 and the nineteen provinces, with the report's own provisional areas (暫定面積), its own sex ratios and its own densities. Everything was checked against the report's own arithmetic before it was used: the twenty rows sum to the printed 43,202,880 and to the printed area of 1,303,143.252 km² to the last of three decimals, male plus female is the total in every row, and both derived columns recompute from the printed figures to within 0.05. The map draws fourteen of these provinces, not nineteen — its Manchukuo is traced from a 1935 sheet, and 通化, 北安, 東安, 四平 and 牡丹江 were made out of those fourteen afterwards, none of them from a single parent, so they cannot be added back. Six of the fourteen actually lost ground — 奉天, 安東, 濱江, 龍江, 三江 and 吉林, which is where those five were cut from — and the rest are unchanged. The fourteen shapes hold 35,695,802 of the 43,202,880 with the capital among them; the other 7,507,078 are in the five provinces that have no shape, which are in the table without one. 新京特別市 is added into 吉林省, the capital being a special municipality by 1943 but standing on ground inside the 吉林 this map draws, so that the shape and the figure cover the same ground; the dot on 新京 keeps the city's own 555,009 and is marked as counted inside it. Every province card says in one sentence that its outline is georeferenced from a 1935 map, and the six that the later provinces were taken out of are left blank rather than shaded — a 1943 density drawn on any of them would spread its people over ground that had stopped being theirs. The eight that kept every acre are shaded, on the densities the report itself prints, over the areas it itself prints and not over the polygons. The choropleth reproduces the report's own five classes (5, 20, 40 and 100 to the square kilometre) rather than fitting a ladder of its own; the plate's sixth class is 新京特別市 alone at 1,268.2, which this map draws as a point. The nationality table gives Manchukuo whole — 滿洲人 with its Manchu, Han, Mongolian and Hui divisions, 日本人 with its Japanese, Korean and other, 第三國人 and 無國籍人 — every one of which balances on its parts and on male plus female. Its province rows are not transcribed.

Projection. The map is drawn on a Pacific-centred frame running 66°E–206°E and 13°S–55°N — British India to Pearl Harbor, Sakhalin to northern Australia. Longitudes are normalised to 0–360 so the map is continuous across the antimeridian. Three projections are offered in Layers, and the map is re-projected in the browser rather than re-fetched:

  • Web Mercator, the sheet as built and the one the map opens on.
  • Albers equal-area conic, on 117.5°E with standard parallels at 12.5°N and 37.5°N.
  • Lambert azimuthal equal area, centred on 25°N 115°E.

Transport

Taiwan Railways These were primarily based on the 日治時期鐵路分布圖 (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 Taiwan) 0703b which was reprojected to TWD97. The map seems to align quite closely with historical maps where drawn. However, this map was missing several things needed for the 1930 and 1942 maps. The Hualien to Taidong line was added for both 1930 and 1942. The Hualien 1939 port extension for the 1942 map and a missing coastal station was added for 海岸驛 at 南濱 for the 1930 map. Part of the 宜蘭 line in the north was missing, as was its branch line to 菁桐坑. The 集集線 and 明治製糖南投線 departing east out of 二水 were also added to both maps. All of these were traced from 1944 1944-美軍地形圖-1:25,000 : AM25K_1944A. 東港 line and 溪州/南州—枋寮 lines in the south are kept for 1942, removed for 1930 map. There are a variety of lines found on railway maps that are lines for sugar refineries and mines but some of these carried passengers. This dataset may need to be updated to take that in to account and better represent the full network.

Korea Railways The Korean railways make use of the 근대 철도 DB dataset by 김종혁. 「근대 철도 DB」, 2027년 8월. The lines were filtered for those created before 1931 for the 1930 map and those created before 1943 for the 1942 map. The stations were clipped as needed for each. The dataset has not yet been closely checked with contemporary maps for accuracy. Japanese names were romanized through a combination of best-guess automated romanization and looking for entries online through Japanese Wikipedia etc. This work needs to be done more methodically from scratch for better accuracy. Hangul names were romanized for McCune-Reischauer.

Train Tools, Taiwan 1936 The working timetable behind the Train Tools is the transcription published at kmlawson.github.io/taiwan-1936-timetable, taken from the February 1936 timetable of the Railway Department of the Governor-General of Taiwan (臺灣總督府交通局鐵道部), which can be read at the Internet Archive. It carries 346 trains over the seven passenger lines, with 5,372 calls between them, and the track between consecutive stops. The eighteen printed tables are reproduced whole at the timetable page, and a station's card links to the table for its line. That page reads in English, Chinese or Japanese at the press of a button — the title, the links, the legend and the headings, but never the tables, which are a transcription and are left as printed; the column headings are translated in English only, with the printed word kept on the cell. Station names there carry their reading on a second line, kana in Japanese and Mandarin with tone marks otherwise, from the same station table the map uses.

Station readings Japanese readings are only ever taken from a source that states them in kana, never worked out from the characters — 萬里橋 is Maribashi and 鹿野 is Shikano, and no amount of on-yomi gets you there. tools/fetch_tw_station_readings.py takes the furigana from the lede of a ja.wikipedia station article and converts the kana by table; where there is no article, or none with furigana, the reading is left empty and marked unverified rather than guessed. 「駅名読み方大全」 is the other source consulted: it gives a 駅名一覧表 with kana for every station on a line, and it covers Karafuto — 樺太東線, 樺太西線, 川上線 and 豊真線 — from which 敷香 しくか and 真岡 まおか are taken. Its 台湾地区 and 朝鮮地区 sections exist but are empty as of September 2026, so Taiwan's fifty-two unread stations are still waiting on a source.

Train Tools, Korea 1938 The Korean network behind the Train Tools is transcribed from 朝鮮列車時刻表 (1938), a 朝鮮・滿洲・內地 pocket timetable carrying the revisions of December 1937 and January 1938, which can be read at the Internet Archive. It carries 1,666 trains over 74 lines calling at 1,302 stations, 695 of them placed from the NIKH historical GIS of Korean railways (checked by 김종혁), with the track between consecutive stops traced along the 1942 line geometry. The 173 printed tables are reproduced whole at the timetable page, and a station's card links to the table for its line. The Manchurian and Japanese pages and the four ferries are carried as the network the Korean tables connect to: their stations sit at the point of a city this map already draws and the track between them is a straight chord, so they are drawn faint, say so on their cards, and are off until the reader asks for them. The pages were transcribed by Claude Code's vision model and have not yet been checked by a human; that is planned, and corrections are welcome. Air routes The Tokyo–Dairen trunk on the 1930 sheet is timed from the company's own 昭和五年四月改正 定期航空発着時刻及賃金表 (April 1930): two days each way with a night at Keijō in both directions, no service on Sundays, and the Osaka and Fukuoka calls made by seaplane at Kizugawajiri and Najima. Its fare table is the one this map carries — all twenty-one figures agree with the reading taken from 酒井正子's article, which is a check on both. The other services are drawn from 酒井正子「変容する世界の航空界・その4 日本の航空100年」, which reproduces the Japan Air Transport network and, on p101, the October 1938 – March 1939 timetable, citing 『航空輸送の歩み』大日本航空社史刊行会編, 1975, p65. The Tokyo–Dairen trunk's 1931 times are from the summer diagram in the same article; the fares from the same source add up leg by leg, which is what proves the reading of a printed triangle whose rows and columns are only implied by position. The Fukuoka–Naha–Taihoku line's opening and its first three-day-a-week schedule are from JACAR C05034292500 海軍省-公文備考-S10-78-4892 (1935), and a 1938 note on its aircraft, fares and the mail-only Tainan–Makō leg from 旅程と費用概算, p893. The Yokohama–Saipan–Palau flying boat's April 1939 schedule is from JACAR C01007343400 陸軍省-大日記乙輯-S14-13-41. The Dutch networks on the 1942 sheet are two documents, both older than the map they are drawn on. The KLM trunk from Amsterdam is the summer service of 29 March 1938, which the frame catches from Karachi eastward — four days to Bandoeng, leaving Karachi on Mondays, Thursdays and Saturdays. The twenty-six KNILM lines across the Indies — twenty-five inside them and one out to Manila — are from the Complete Map of the Airlines of K.N.I.L.M. of about 1935, which is a route map and not a timetable: the lines are drawn and nothing is said about when they flew. By December 1942 neither network was running — the Japanese had taken the Indies that spring — and each card says so. They are drawn to show what had been there. The 1930 sheet carries three lines of the China National Aviation Corporation (中國航空公司), the Nationalist government's joint venture with Pan American: the lower Yangtze from Shanghai to Hankow, on up through the gorges to Chungking, and north up the coast to Peiping. They are read from the company's own timetable of about 1933 — the Yangtze services and the Chungking and Peiping routes — so they are three years later than the sheet they are drawn on, which is the nearest schedule found. On the Chungking line the source gives two departures a week and three returns, and the card says so. Three European lines are here too. Air Orient's Marseilles–Saigon service, from its summer 1931 sheet, and Imperial Airways' India end from 16 May 1931, both on the 1930 map; and on the 1942 one, Imperial's August 1939 services to Darwin and to Calcutta, and Air France's 1938 line to Hong Kong. The last two run on across ground that was Japanese-held by December 1942 — Rangoon, Singapore, Saigon, Hanoi — and those stretches are drawn faint, kept out of the animation and still pressable, because leaving them off would say the network stopped where in fact only the sheet stops. Air Orient's and Air France's clocks are inferred from distance and are marked as such on every call. Indian National Airways is on both sheets from the same collection of timetables: the Calcutta–Rangoon coast run and the Calcutta–Dacca shuttle on the 1930 map, from the sheet of 10 December 1933, and the Delhi–Karachi trunk down the Indus on the 1942 one, from November 1938. Their clocks are Indian standard time, as every line here keeps the local time of the country that flew it. The 1930 sheet also carries two lines outside the Japanese and Chinese networks: the Philippine Aerial Taxi Company's hour from Manila up to the hill station at Baguio, from an undated brochure of about the mid-1930s — its card names the Paracale, INAEC and Philippine Air Lines services that followed and are not drawn — and the Aerial Transport Company of Siam's mail line up the Khorat plateau to the Mekong, from its 1933 timetable, which is two services on one line and is drawn as two. A fifth 1930 line runs down the whole China coast — Shanghai to Canton by Wenchow, Foochow, Amoy and Swatow — which opened in October 1933 and is timed here from the company's 1935 schedule, the one found. Three more CNAC lines are on the 1942 sheet, from the company's October 1940 timetable: Chungking to Rangoon by Kunming and Lashio — the air equivalent of the Burma Road, and the one route out of free China once the coast had gone — and two provincial services, the Chengtu shuttle and the line up to Kiating. Three lines of the Manchuria Aviation Company (満洲航空株式会社) are on the 1942 sheet from the company's winter 1935 timetable: the trunk from Harbin down through Shinkyō and Mukden to Dairen; the west line out along the Chinese Eastern Railway to the Soviet frontier at Manchouli, which is really two services and is drawn as two; and the pre-dawn run from Shinkyō to Shingishū that was the Manchurian end of the through service to Tokyo. Seven years earlier than the sheet, and each card says so. The four China Airways lines on the 1942 sheet — Peking to Shanghai, to Dairen, west to Paotow, and the Yangtze services to Hankow — are read from the company's own 1940 timetable. 中華航空株式會社 was set up in December 1938, a joint concern of 大日本航空 and the Japanese-sponsored governments at Peking and Nanking. The brochure prints its places in characters only; 安慶 Anking is identified from the distance column, whose three Yangtze legs sum to the figure given for the nonstop and match the real spacing to within five per cent. Tientsin is marked as a call on two of the lines with no clock against it, and the times drawn there are inferred from the printed ends and distances — each card says which. The whole transcription is in data/air/README.md. Also consulted: Mizusawa Hikari, "The Civilian Air Transportation Network that Linked Japan with Its Colonies", JACAR.

The July 1942 sheet. The aviation pages (118–121) of 満州支那汽車時間表 (Manshū Shina kisha jikanhyō), Japan's railway timetable for Manchuria, July 1942 issue (昭和17年7月號), photographed for this project, carry 滿洲航空's tables as revised on 1 June 1942 and 中華航空's as revised on 1 April 1942 — thirty-four lines, from the Shinkyō–Tokyo through flight to the Amur frontier posts of Huma and Mohe. They are on the 1942 map beside the winter-1935 and 1940 readings, not in their place, and every one is marked as that sheet's: the company string on the chooser and the card ends "July 1942 sheet" or "April 1942 sheet", the season names the 改正 stamp, and each has an ink of its own. Nine of the tables are headed 運休 (suspended); those are drawn dimmed with their printed times and are not flown. data/air/README.md has the reading, and its two awkward tables — a zigzag through Paoching, a misprinted distance — are explained there.

Download the geometry

The map's own shapes, in lon/lat as GeoJSON, for opening in QGIS or anything else. These are written before projection and before any thinning — they are what the map means rather than what it happens to look like at one scale — and they carry the atom key and the unit name, so a file can be joined to the tables above.

  • Administrative units — 550 units across 46 countries and colonies: the provinces, prefectures, 州廳, districts and states the Administrative layer draws. 13 MB.
  • Land — 84 outlines, one per atom: the countries, colonies and island groups as whole shapes, without their internal divisions. 7 MB.
  • The occupied zone, December 1942 — five blocks, traced from a period map and adjusted. Approximate, and generous: Japanese control ran along the railways and around the cities. Clip it to the land to get what the map draws.
  • The Yellow River before 1938 — the bed it held from 1855, reaching the sea through the Gulf of Chihli.
  • The Yellow River, 1938–1947 — after the dikes were cut at Huayuankou in June 1938: down the Chia-lu into the Ying, the Ying into the Huai, and through Hungtse Lake and the Grand Canal into the Yangtze.
  • The Yangtze

The shaded relief

Not geometry — nine images, one for each of three levels of detail in each of the map's three projections, and the map fetches whichever pair the reader's zoom and projection ask for. They are here as direct downloads because there is nothing else to hand over: a raster has no shapes to write.

They are already projected, so each is only true of the projection in its name; the extent is the map's own frame. Derived from public-domain elevation data — see the note above on the relief.

For a single shape, ask the map. Right-click any polygon (or press and hold on a touch screen) and it offers that unit, its group where it has one, and the whole layer it belongs to — along with the coordinates under the pointer and a short note on where the shape came from. Those files are the drawn geometry and carry the thinning the map draws at; the files above do not, which is why both exist.
